**Top find this hour:** China’s first group standard *AI OPC Terminology* has been formally released, drawing a clear boundary for the one-person company — controlled by a single core individual, generally no more than 10 employees, with AI research/development/application as core business.

- **Group standard released (Tencent News, 2026-07-09):** The *AI OPC Terminology* group standard defines an AI OPC as a company led and controlled by one core individual, generally with ≤10 employees, whose main business is AI research, development, application or services. The core individual’s personal brand and professional capability are the primary assets, using intelligent agents for independent operation and scalable delivery.
- **Local policy tailwinds (CSDN, 2026-07-09):** After China’s revised Company Law loosened rules, individuals can set up unlimited “one-person companies” and even “sub one-person companies.” Shenzhen Nanshan/Bao’an launched dedicated OPC communities; Shanghai Lin-gang rolled out a “Super Individual 288” plan with free rent and support. Solo developers now handle full-stack dev, multi-tenant architecture and dual payment integration via tools like Claude Code.
- **Beike Finance Annual Summit (Tencent News, 2026-07-15):** The 2026 Beike Finance consumer summit (Beijing, July 10) debuted a “Youth Entrepreneurship Open Mic” unit. The OPC model is moving from concept to practice as a new path for young founders.
- **First OPC-AI bootcamp in Ningbo (Tencent News, 2026-07-12):** The course centers on AIGC and the OpenClaw agent system, empowering individuals to build a “person + AI army” productivity model to boost the regional startup ecosystem.
- **Analyst view — Xu Qiuli (China Informatization Weekly, 2026-07-13):** OPC has moved beyond traditional freelancing. Its core form uses a super-individual or IP as a trust entry point to link resources and win business, then delivers with a small, elite collaborative team. He cautions that practitioners must think about long-term survival.
- **“Betting big on the one-person company” (CSDN, 2026-07-09):** An indie developer’s 2026 review cites the shutdown of no-code tool Bento, a 5% preferential tax rate in some regions, and AI now writing 41% of code. With Claude Code, one person can do full-stack dev, database design and multi-tenant architecture.
- **OPC core formula (CSDN, 2026-07-10):** 1 person + 100 agents = 1 company. The OPC is not “one person doing everything,” but an individual orchestrating AI models, agents/Skills, content IP, product delivery and collaboration networks. The CEO needs seven capabilities: strategy, content, IP, brand, product, tech and operations.
- **Mature paradigm (CSDN, 2026-07-13):** AI tools have shifted from assistants to programmable, collaborative, systematic productivity units. Solo founders combining AI “super employees” (AI agents), automation tool chains and cloud services can efficiently run a micro-enterprise covering the full business chain, with near-zero content creation cost.
- **Korea’s Upstage forms Upstage Company (2026-06-16):** AI startup Upstage plans to merge its AI models, agent services and portal platform into a new entity, Upstage Company. CEO Kim Sung-hoon previously acquired portal Daum and AI agent platform Timely AI — reflecting the global trend of small teams scaling fast through AI-agent consolidation.
- **No-code agent tool Shipable (SourceForge, 2026-07-13):** Shipable is a no-code AI agent platform for agencies and consultants to quickly build and deploy support/sales/lead-gen assistants, with multilingual workflows and payment/domain embedding. It scales from solo operators to studios ($35/mo) — a low-barrier, solopreneur-friendly tool.
- **Overseas solopreneur ecosystem (2026-07):** Ayalr builds monitoring tools and AI systems for solopreneurs; LifeStarr (founded by the *Solopreneur Business for Dummies* authors) offers community, tools and education for one-person founders — reflecting mature Western demand for lean solo operations.
- **Cold take (Tencent News, 2026-06-23):** Amid the OPC hype, some practitioners warn: AI is just a tool. A one-person company must return to business fundamentals — who you serve, what problem you solve, and how you make money — rather than chasing tech trends blindly.
