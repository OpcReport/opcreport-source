Three storylines converged this week that every solo builder should re-run their numbers against: Anthropic shipped a near-flagship model at half the price, Andrew Ng open-sourced a desktop agent that hit 7.6k GitHub stars in five days, and Monday.com became the 21st major tech company this year to cite AI while cutting staff. Together they answer the two questions this report always asks: **where to build** (the agent supervision layer, and vertical agents that absorb the jobs SaaS companies are cutting) and **what to build with** (Opus 5 for heavy reasoning, cheap fast models for bulk work, and an orchestration layer you can swap).

## Key Insights

- **Claude Opus 5 launched July 24 at $10/$50 per Mtok (fast mode) with a 1M context window** — roughly half the price of flagship-tier models, and it’s already the default Opus model in Claude Code v2.1.219. If you shelved a multi-agent workflow because inference was too expensive, that math just changed.
- **The bottleneck is shifting from building agents to supervising them.** OpenWorker (Andrew Ng’s open-source desktop agent, built on his provider-agnostic aisuite framework) gained 7.6k stars in 5 days; WhipDesk (open-source, control Claude Code/Codex/Copilot/Cursor from your phone, E2E encrypted, scheduled prompts) is climbing fast. Demand for the “agent fleet dashboard” is validated — and there’s no mature commercial product in the space.
- **Monday.com cut ~20% of staff (620 people) while still guiding 20% revenue growth.** The pattern across 21 companies this year: mid-size SaaS is shedding implementation, support, and customer-success roles — exactly the “human glue” functions vertical agents can absorb, and exactly the talent pool that will swell the solopreneur ranks.

## Market Trends

### The inference price war hits “flagship at half price”

Opus 5’s pitch is blunt: intelligence close to the top-tier Fable 5, at half the cost. On CursorBench 3.2 with max effort, it scores within 0.5% of Fable 5’s peak at half the per-task cost; against its own predecessor Opus 4.8, Anthropic claims doubled Frontier-Bench performance at lower per-task cost. It ships with 1M context and is now the Claude Code default.

What this means in practice:

- Heavy agent coding sessions that used to run $10–20 now cost roughly half for the same output. Running 3–5 sub-agents in parallel — each in its own directory, human only doing acceptance review (a workflow builders are already demoing in Cursor) — moves from extravagant to routine.
- 1M context means small-to-mid codebases fit whole. Repo-wide refactors, audits, and doc generation need far less RAG glue.
- Strategic takeaway: the model layer is competing on your behalf. Don’t bet on a vendor — keep your stack swappable and re-price your roadmap quarterly.

### From building agents to managing them

When every builder runs multiple agents concurrently, the pain moves up a layer: *what are they doing, what did they break, and can I check from my phone?* OpenWorker (macOS live, Windows coming) and WhipDesk both attack this from the open-source side — remote control, one-screen visibility across Claude Code, Codex, Copilot and Cursor, scheduled runs.

What’s missing — and open for a solo builder — is the commercial layer: cost tracking per agent, quality gates, alerting, team seats. Big labs will eventually ship first-party dashboards, but cross-vendor, mobile-first, cost-accounting tooling is precisely what they won’t prioritize.

### SaaS layoffs are quietly stocking the solopreneur economy

Monday.com’s SEC filing frames its 20% cut as “reorganizing for an AI-first vision,” not replacement — with $45–55M in restructuring charges and unchanged growth guidance. Two actionable reads:

1. The roles being cut (implementation, onboarding, support ops) map one-to-one onto vertical agent products you can sell back to the same companies.
2. Thousands of experienced product/engineering/CS people are entering the market; many will go solo. The tool stack that serves them — monitoring (Ayalr), SEO (MetaBoost), project acquisition (Little Exits) — is a growing lane with obvious gaps left.

## Risk Factors

- **Model churn:** any business built on today’s pricing must survive another 50% price cut. Your moat is workflow and distribution, never the model call.
- **Supervision-layer window is finite:** Anthropic/OpenAI will ship first-party dashboards. Position where they won’t go — cross-vendor, mobile, cost accounting.
- **Vertical agent integration tax:** selling agents to SMB SaaS, your real cost is integration with messy customer data, not development. Validate with 3 design partners before scaling.
- **Fast-follower crowding:** post-Opus-5, “whole-repo audit” products will flood in within 60 days. Only niche depth (specific language/framework) differentiates.
