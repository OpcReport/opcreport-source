# Inference Costs $0.14. Your Throughput Still Only Moved 8%

**July 31, 2026**

---

## Key Insights

Two numbers published within 48 hours of each other explain the current market better than any forecast.

The first: **DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 went to general availability today at $0.14 per million input tokens and $0.28 per million output**, with a 1M context window, 384K max output, and cache-hit input at **$0.0028**. This is not a toy tier. Its published agent benchmarks are **Terminal Bench 2.1: 82.7**, **Toolathlon verified: 70.3**, **DeepSWE: 54.4**, **Cybergym: 76.7**, **Agents’ Last Exam: 25.2**. It natively speaks the Responses API and ships a documented Codex integration. Combined with yesterday’s 80% cut to GPT-5.6 Luna, the price of competent tool-calling has collapsed by roughly an order of magnitude in a single week.

The second: **7.76%.** That is the median increase in pull-request throughput measured across 400+ companies from November 2024 to February 2026, during a period when AI tool adoption at those same companies rose **65%**. Mean gain 13.1%. Even the 90th percentile landed near 44%. The study’s explanation is blunt: **coding is roughly 16% of how engineers spend their time**, so halving it moves almost nothing, and the time saved writing code gets eaten by the extra scrutiny AI output requires.

Put those side by side and the strategic picture is unambiguous. **Generation is now essentially free and essentially solved. The bottleneck moved downstream — to review, integration, verification, context and supervision — and almost nobody is selling into it.**

Everything else worth noticing this week points the same way:

- The most-starred new repos are not models. They are **agent skills** — 739 stars for a skill that forces docs into Simplified Technical English to kill AI slop, 568 stars in three days for one that makes an agent behave like a lazy senior dev who refuses to write unnecessary code. Zero infrastructure, pure judgment, sold as a text file.
- **A 2.78-trillion-parameter model now runs on a single machine** by streaming activated weights from NVMe. Two independent projects shipped this in three days.
- Session state is quietly becoming the new lock-in. OpenAI’s Responses API stores responses **at least 30 days by default**; Gemini’s Interactions API defaults to `store: true` with **55-day** paid retention. Your reasoning traces come back as encrypted blobs you cannot read and cannot port.
- Meanwhile the AI capital markets are getting repriced hard. Aschenbrenner’s Situational Awareness fund was forced to unwind its public equity book after heavy AI losses, with Citadel buying the portfolio. The financing environment for anything speculative just tightened.

**Numbers that should change your decisions:**

- **$0.14 / $0.28 per million tokens** with Terminal Bench 2.1 at 82.7. If you are still routing bulk classification, test generation or log triage to a frontier model, you are burning margin for no measurable quality gain.
- **65% adoption increase → 7.76% throughput increase.** If you are selling “10x developer productivity,” your buyer now has a 400-company counter-study. Reprice your pitch around the actual bottleneck.
- **Coding is ~16% of engineering time.** Meaning ~84% of the workday is a market that AI coding tools currently do not touch.
- **739 / 568 / 512 / 508 stars in under a week** for four projects with no infrastructure, no funding and no team. Distribution for solo builders is working better than it has in years — as long as the artifact is opinionated.

The synthesis: **stop competing on generation and start selling everything that has to happen after generation.** The models got cheap enough that your cost structure no longer decides whether you survive. Your position in the workflow does.

## Market Trends

### 1. The cheap tier caught up on tool use specifically — which changes routing, not strategy

Historically the cheap models were cheap because they were worse at everything. That has stopped being uniformly true. DeepSeek-V4-Flash’s gains are concentrated exactly where agent work lives: terminal operation, tool orchestration, repo-scale code changes. Terminal Bench 2.1 at 82.7 and Toolathlon verified at 70.3 are not frontier-adjacent numbers on trivia — they are numbers on *doing things*.

Two practical consequences.

First, **tiered routing is now mandatory, not clever**. Use the expensive model for planning, ambiguity, architecture and anything where being wrong is expensive to discover. Use the $0.14 tier for implementation, test writing, log analysis, classification, migration passes and evaluation runs. The quality delta on well-specified mechanical work no longer justifies a 10-30x price difference.

Second, and less discussed: **DeepSeek is introducing peak/off-peak pricing at 2x regular rates during 09:00–12:00 and 14:00–18:00 Beijing time**, across all billing items. If your workload is batch — nightly evals, bulk content generation, index rebuilds — schedule it outside those windows and your COGS halves. If your product runs interactive agents for Asian users, model that 2x into your unit economics before you set a subscription price. This is the first mainstream inference provider treating tokens like electricity, and it will not be the last.

The strategic read, though, is the uncomfortable one. When a capable agent model costs 14 cents per million tokens, **“we call a model for you” has no defensible margin**. Anything that a price cut can commoditize, a price cut will commoditize — and there were two of them this week.

### 2. The productivity gap is where the money is, and it is enormous

The DX longitudinal study is the most commercially useful thing published this week, precisely because it is unflattering.

Adoption up 65%. Median PR throughput up 7.76%. The developers interviewed described the mechanism plainly: *“A four-day task might take three. But that doesn’t mean I’m shipping 3x more PRs.”* Time saved on generation is consumed by review, because AI-generated output requires more scrutiny, not less. Code review and integration remain largely unassisted. And AI performs well on self-contained, well-documented problems — which describes almost nothing in a real codebase.

This is not a story about AI disappointing. It is a **map of unserved demand**. Every organization in that sample is now paying 65% more for tooling and receiving 8% more output. The delta between those two numbers is the total addressable market for anyone who can attack the actual constraint.

And the actual constraint is not intelligence. It is:

- **Review throughput** — one human cannot review machine-volume diffs
- **Institutional context** — the model does not know why your billing module is weird
- **Acceptance criteria** — nobody wrote down what “done” means, so nothing can be verified automatically
- **Integration** — the generated code works in isolation and breaks in the system

Notice that none of these get solved by the next model release. They get solved by tooling built around today’s capabilities. That is a product category, and it is being addressed almost entirely by individuals right now.

Supporting signal from an unlikely direction: Google reported fixing more Chrome security bugs in June than in the previous two years combined, attributing it to AI. That is a real 10x — but it happened in *triage and detection*, a bounded, verifiable, high-volume task with objective acceptance criteria. That is the shape of work where AI actually delivers order-of-magnitude gains. Shape your product to that shape.

### 3. Agent skills became the highest-leverage distribution format for solo builders

Look at what is climbing GitHub fastest this week and notice what these projects are *not*: they are not services, not SaaS, not infrastructure. They are opinions, packaged.

- **SimpleEnglish** — an agent skill that forces documentation into ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English to eliminate AI slop. **739 stars.** The entire product is a constraint applied to writing.
- **ponytail-improved** — “makes your AI agent think like the laziest senior dev in the room. The best code is the code you never wrote.” **568 stars in three days.** The product is a philosophy about not writing code.
- **ai-design-skills** — 240 stars, a skills bundle for design work.
- **wechat-exporter** — 180 stars in three days, a Claude Code / Codex skill that exports and decrypts local chat history to SQLite. The product is one hard, specific, unglamorous integration.
- **Black-cat** — a hypothesis-driven red-team skill, 129 stars.

Marginal cost of distribution: zero. Infrastructure: none. Time to v1: days. And the thing being sold is the one input models cannot generate for themselves — **taste and constraint**.

The obvious objection is that a markdown file has no moat, and that is true. But it is also the wrong frame. A skill is not the business; it is the **top of funnel with the best conversion rate available to an individual right now**. Ship the skill free, let it earn 500 stars in a week, and sell the harness, the hosted version, the team layer or the update stream underneath it.

Related and worth watching: a Show HN this week asked “what should the GUI for AI agents look like?” and drew 68 points on the premise that files, tools, tasks and outputs should be visible in a workspace rather than buried in chat threads. Meanwhile another builder shipped agent supervision to Telegram to stop babysitting sessions. The interface layer for agent work is genuinely unsettled, which is exactly when a small builder can define the convention.

### 4. Local inference crossed from “impressive” to “structurally interesting”

Two projects shipped in the same three-day window:

- **deltafin** — runs full Kimi K3 on a single device, with an OpenAI-compatible API server for local chat and coding agents. **512 stars.**
- **waste** — a dependency-free, embeddable C engine that runs the full **2.78-trillion-parameter** Kimi K3 beyond available RAM by streaming activated weights directly from NVMe. **110 stars**, actively pushed today.

Follow the implication rather than the benchmark. A trillion-scale open model running on hardware someone already owns means the **gross margin on a local-first application is near 100%, permanently**. No inference bill means no per-user cost floor, which means one-time pricing works, which means you are not selling a subscription you must justify every month.

It also means privacy-constrained markets — legal, clinical, therapeutic, journalistic, financial compliance — become addressable by a single developer with no enterprise sales motion and no data processing agreement to negotiate. The buyer’s objection was always “where does my data go,” and the answer is now “nowhere.”

### 5. The financing climate turned, which makes bootstrapping the default rather than the fallback

Leopold Aschenbrenner’s Situational Awareness fund — the most visible pure AI-thesis vehicle in public markets — was forced to unwind its public stock positions after steep losses, with Citadel picking up the portfolio. Coverage this week describes the AI trade as increasingly running on borrowed money, with lenders repricing that debt. A judge is separately questioning whether the US government justified its ban on Anthropic.

None of this means AI demand is collapsing; enterprise usage is plainly still growing. What it means for you is narrower and more actionable: **capital for speculative AI businesses is getting more expensive and more selective right now.** Plans that depend on a seed round in Q4, on a partner’s credits staying free, or on a growth-before-revenue window staying open should be stress-tested this week.

The counterweight is real, though: new business formation in the US remains strong enough that The Economist ran a leader this week arguing the startup boom offers genuine hope for the future of work. Formation is up. Funding is down. That combination has one obvious strategy attached to it — **revenue from month one, from a small number of customers who pay because the thing works**.

## Build Directions

Five things you could start this week, with the stack and honest economics.

### 1. Review and integration intelligence — sell into the 92% that AI did not fix

**What:** Tooling that attacks the actual bottleneck the DX data exposed. PR risk scoring for AI-generated diffs. Automatic extraction of acceptance criteria from tickets before generation starts. Integration-surface analysis that flags where a generated change touches systems the model could not see. Review queue triage that tells one maintainer which of eleven open PRs actually needs human eyes.

**Why now:** The productivity gap is measured, published, and 400+ companies wide. Buyers who felt vaguely disappointed now have a number for their disappointment, which makes them receptive in a way they were not six months ago.

**Stack:** GitHub App via Octokit + TypeScript, webhook on PR open. Route bulk diff classification to DeepSeek-V4-Flash at $0.14/M — this is exactly the well-specified mechanical work the cheap tier is now good at — and escalate only flagged diffs to a frontier model. Store findings in Postgres, surface as PR comments and a weekly digest.

**Economics:** 3–5 weeks to v1. **$19–49 per seat per month**, or $199–499/month per repo for teams. Your inference cost at the cheap tier is trivially small relative to that, so gross margin is comfortable from the first customer.

**Sharpest wedge:** don’t build “AI code review” — that space is crowded with generic linters. Build **“which of these AI-generated PRs is safe to merge without reading,”** which is the question the buyer actually has at 6pm on a Friday.

### 2. Session portability and agent audit vaults

**What:** A layer that captures a complete, provider-independent record of every agent session — instructions, messages, tool calls, tool results, retrieved evidence — and can replay it against a different model or provider. Plus deletion tracking: which server-side copies exist, and where.

**Why now:** The lock-in surface moved from weights to state, and almost nobody has priced this in. OpenAI’s Responses API retains response objects **at least 30 days by default**; Gemini’s Interactions API defaults to `store: true` at **55-day** retention on paid tiers. Reasoning comes back as encrypted blobs the client cannot read; hosted search means the model saw source material you never will. A response ID is not a transcript — it is a foreign key into a database you do not control. Every regulated buyer will eventually need an answer to “show me why the agent did that,” and today most cannot produce one.

**Stack:** TypeScript SDK wrapping the OpenAI Responses and Anthropic APIs with `store: false` enforced by default. Client-side tool execution instead of hosted tools wherever possible, so search results land in your own log. SQLite or Postgres for the transcript store, S3 for artifacts, one-command export to a portable JSON archive.

**Economics:** 4–6 weeks. **$99–499/month per team**, higher for regulated verticals where the audit trail is a compliance line item rather than a nice-to-have. Slower sales cycle than #1, but far stickier once installed.

**Test your own exposure first:** can you export a session, revoke your provider credentials, and continue the work elsewhere? If not, you have a dependency you did not choose.

### 3. Agent skills as a product line

**What:** Ship opinionated agent skills — narrow, forceful constraints on how an agent works — free and public, then monetize the layer underneath. The pattern is proven repeatedly this week at 500–700 stars in days.

**Why now:** It is the cheapest distribution available to an individual, and the artifact rewards exactly what a solo builder has that a funded team does not: a strong, specific opinion. SimpleEnglish is a writing standard. ponytail is a stance about not writing code. Neither required infrastructure.

**Stack:** Markdown skill definitions for Claude Code / Codex, published on GitHub with a genuinely good README and a five-line install. Then build the paid layer: a hosted runner, a team config sync, a verified update channel, or a CI action that enforces the skill on every PR.

**Economics:** Days to v1 — this is the fastest path on this list. Free skill, then **$9–29/month** for the enforcement or hosting layer, or a **$99 one-time** pro bundle. Realistic ceiling is modest ($2–5K MRR), but the time-to-signal is measured in days rather than months, which makes it an excellent way to test a market before committing a quarter to it.

**The trap:** a markdown file forks in seconds. Never let the skill *be* the product. Let it be the reason someone finds you.

### 4. Local-first applications for privacy-bound work

**What:** Desktop applications running open models entirely on-device for buyers who cannot send data anywhere — transcription, document review, case notes, contract analysis, private research.

**Why now:** The last technical objection died this week. If a 2.78T-parameter model can stream from NVMe on one machine, and full Kimi K3 runs on a single device with an OpenAI-compatible local server, then “the good models need a datacenter” is no longer a true statement you can plan around.

**Stack:** Swift + MLX on Apple Silicon, or llama.cpp for cross-platform. Point it at a local OpenAI-compatible endpoint (deltafin-style) so you can swap models without touching application code. Distribute through the Mac App Store, or direct with Paddle or Lemon Squeezy.

**Economics:** 4–8 weeks. **$49–149 one-time**, near-100% gross margin, zero marginal cost per user. The absence of a subscription is a selling point to this buyer, not a weakness in your model.

### 5. Revenue-first analytics for micro-SaaS operators

**What:** Analytics built around money rather than pageviews — revenue attribution, per-customer revenue integration, session replay tied to a paying account rather than an anonymous visitor. An open-source, self-hosted project in this exact category took **508 stars in two days** this week, which is a market speaking clearly.

**Why now:** The population of one-person software businesses is expanding fast, and the existing analytics stack was built for marketing teams optimizing funnels at scale. A solo operator with 200 customers does not need cohort heatmaps. They need to know which of those 200 is about to churn and which acquisition channel produced the three that pay the most.

**Stack:** Next.js dashboard, Postgres or ClickHouse for events, direct integrations with Stripe, Paddle and Lemon Squeezy. Self-hostable core plus a hosted tier — the self-host option is what earns you the GitHub stars that become your distribution.

**Economics:** 5–8 weeks. **$29–99/month** hosted; free self-hosted as the funnel. Crowded-looking category with a genuinely underserved segment, so niche it hard — pick “AI-tool micro-SaaS” or “developer tools sold to individuals” and win that specific buyer completely.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Review & integration intelligence | 3–5 weeks | $19–49/seat/mo | GitHub or the model vendor ships it natively |
| Session portability & audit | 4–6 weeks | $99–499/mo team | Slow, compliance-paced sales cycle |
| Agent skills product line | Days | Free + $9–29/mo | Trivially forkable; low ceiling alone |
| Local-first apps | 4–8 weeks | $49–149 one-time | No recurring revenue; hardware-bound market |
| Revenue-first analytics | 5–8 weeks | $29–99/mo | Crowded unless tightly niched |

## Risk Factors

**Peak pricing turns a fixed cost into a variable one.** DeepSeek is moving to 2x rates during 09:00–12:00 and 14:00–18:00 Beijing time across all billing items. If your agent workload is interactive and your users are in Asia, your COGS just gained a time-of-day dimension that your flat monthly subscription does not. Model it before you price, and move every batch job you control outside those windows.

**Selling “10x productivity” is now a liability.** The counter-evidence is published, longitudinal and covers 400+ companies at a 7.76% median. Any pitch built on order-of-magnitude claims invites a buyer to send you a link instead of a purchase order. Sell against the measured bottleneck — review, integration, context — and use the 8% number yourself, as the reason your product exists.

**Provider-sealed state is a dependency you did not agree to.** Encrypted reasoning blobs, hosted search you cannot inspect, stored conversations keyed by IDs on someone else’s server. Two consequences: you cannot honestly answer an audit question about why your agent acted, and you cannot migrate providers without losing operational continuity. Set `store: false`, run tools client-side where you can, and keep your own transcript today — retrofitting this after you have 500 customers is expensive.

**Cheap capable models compress your pricing power, not just your costs.** A 10x price drop is only good news if your value sits above the model layer. If your customer can replicate 70% of your product by pointing a $0.14 model at their own data, your pricing has a ceiling you have not measured yet. Run the test deliberately, this week.

**The funding window narrowed while formation stayed high.** More people are starting businesses; less speculative capital is available to fund them. That is a competitive, revenue-disciplined environment. Do not build a plan whose next milestone requires someone else’s balance sheet.

## Action Recommendations

**If you’re starting out:**

- ✅ Ship an opinionated agent skill this week. Days to build, public feedback in 72 hours, and the star count tells you whether anyone cares before you spend a quarter finding out.
- ✅ Pick a problem that lives *after* generation. Review, verification, integration, context, audit. Generation is a solved commodity with two price cuts in one week; the work around it is neither.
- ✅ Default to local-first if your buyer has any privacy constraint at all. Zero marginal cost, one-time pricing, and the hardest objection in the sale disappears.
- ✅ Set up tiered routing on day one. DeepSeek-V4-Flash at $0.14/$0.28 for mechanical work, frontier for planning. Building this habit early is far easier than retrofitting it when your bill hurts.

**If you’re already shipping:**

- ✅ Audit your token spend by task class this week and move everything well-specified to the cheap tier. With Terminal Bench 2.1 at 82.7, the quality argument for paying 10-30x on implementation work no longer holds.
- ✅ Move batch workloads out of 09:00–12:00 and 14:00–18:00 Beijing time before peak pricing lands. This is free money for anyone running nightly evals or bulk generation.
- ✅ Set `store: false` and start keeping full local transcripts including tool results. Do it before a customer asks you to explain an agent action you cannot reconstruct.
- ✅ Rewrite your positioning against the 7.76% number. “We make your team 10x faster” is now checkable and false. “We fix the review bottleneck that ate your 65% adoption increase” is checkable and true.
- ✅ Run the commoditization test: could a competent builder rebuild 70% of your product with a $0.14 model and a weekend? If yes, move up the stack now, while you still have the revenue to fund the move.

## What It Means

The industry spent two years assuming that when models got cheap and good enough, the value would follow automatically. Both halves arrived this week — a genuinely capable agent model at 14 cents per million tokens — and the throughput data says the value did not follow. It stalled at 8%, because the constraint was never generation.

That is the whole opportunity, stated plainly. The expensive part of software was never typing the code. It was knowing what to build, deciding whether the output is correct, fitting it into a system that already exists, and being able to explain afterwards why it did what it did. None of those got cheaper this week. All of them got more urgent, because there is now vastly more machine output flowing into the same narrow human review channel.

Build for the channel, not the flood. The flood is free now.
