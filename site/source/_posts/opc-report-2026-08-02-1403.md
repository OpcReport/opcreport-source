# Same Model, Same Task, 29x the Tokens: Your Harness Is the Real Bill

**August 2, 2026 | For independent developers and solopreneurs**

---

## Key Insights

Two things happened within about 24 hours of each other, and the pairing is almost too neat.

**One.** A developer ran ten agentic tasks in an Ubuntu 26.04 VM, on the same model — GPT-5.6 Sol — through six different agent harnesses, and published the token counts. The spread was not ten or twenty percent. It was **172,807 tokens for the lightest harness against 5,073,137 for the heaviest. A 29x difference for identical work on an identical model.**

| Harness | API total | Cached | Uncached | Output | Cache share of input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| smol | 172,807 | 8,704 | 133,630 | 30,473 | 6.1% |
| Pi | 427,211 | 137,216 | 255,551 | 34,444 | 34.9% |
| OpenCode | 1,564,429 | 1,204,736 | 319,221 | 40,472 | 79.1% |
| Codex | 3,005,744 | 2,649,344 | 303,810 | 52,590 | 89.7% |
| Hermes | 3,856,611 | 3,167,232 | 640,999 | 48,380 | 83.2% |
| Claude Code | 5,073,137 | 4,587,008 | 442,961 | 43,168 | 91.2% |

**Two.** On the same weekend, Cursor **removed dollar cost information from its usage page and CSV export**, leaving raw token counts. The thread hit **318 points and 146 comments**, and the top-voted reactions were not about the feature — they were about exit. One long-time paying customer since 2023: *“Can someone help me understand what value cursor offers in 2026?”* Another: *“It is also simple to move back to VS Code and agent extensions.”*

Read together: **the cost of running an agent is being decided by a layer most people never measure, at the exact moment one major vendor made that cost harder to see.**

### The honest version of the 29x number

The raw multiple is real but it flatters the argument, and the pushback in that thread was correct enough to be worth doing the arithmetic. Cached input reads run roughly 90% below uncached, and the heavy harnesses are heavy precisely *because* they park a large stable prefix in cache. Weighting cached tokens at one-tenth gives a cache-adjusted picture:

| Harness | Effective input (uncached + 0.1 × cached) | Multiple vs lightest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| smol | 134,500 | 1.0x |
| Pi | 269,273 | 2.0x |
| OpenCode | 439,695 | 3.3x |
| Codex | 568,744 | 4.2x |
| Hermes | 957,722 | 7.1x |
| Claude Code | 901,662 | 6.7x |

**The 29x collapses to about 6.7x once you price the cache.** That is the number to plan against — and it is still enormous. A **6.7x swing in your largest variable cost, from a component you can change in an afternoon**, is a bigger lever than any model migration on the table right now.

Two caveats the author himself raised, both of which make the result more useful rather than less. First, prompt caching on GPT-5.6 and later requires a prefix of **at least 1,024 tokens — a strict minimum** — and several of the lightest runs finished under that threshold, so the lean harness’s cache share reads worse than its real behavior. Second, and more important for anyone running long jobs: *“staying below compaction is not only cheaper and faster, it also helps the agent stay on track.”* Token bloat is not only a billing problem. It is a quality problem that shows up as drift on exactly the long-horizon tasks you bought an agent for.

The counter-case deserves airtime too. Heavy harnesses carry that context deliberately — compaction that appends files, tool artifacts, and images is what lets a Codex-class agent run coherently for days. Strip it and you may win the token benchmark on ten short tasks while losing the completion rate on the tasks that actually matter. **The lesson is not “use the smallest harness.” It is “match harness weight to task horizon, and measure instead of assuming.”**

### Numbers that should change a decision

- **29x raw / 6.7x cache-adjusted** — token spread across six harnesses, same model, same tasks
- **6.1% → 91.2%** — cache share of input across those harnesses
- **1,024 tokens** — strict minimum prefix for caching to activate on GPT-5.6+; below it you pay full freight
- **~90%** — discount on cached input reads versus uncached
- **318 points / 146 comments** — reaction to one vendor hiding cost data
- **37,221 stars, 4,822 forks** — a free MIT gateway aggregating 290+ providers and 90+ free tiers

## Market Trends

### 1. The harness became the dominant cost variable while everyone watched model prices

For two years the cost conversation has been about model choice: which model, at what per-token price, for which task. That framing is now roughly one layer too high. When the same model on the same tasks produces a 6.7x cache-adjusted cost swing depending on the wrapper, **your harness selection dominates your model selection as a cost decision.**

The mechanism is context volume. Popular harnesses ship large system prompts, skills files, tool definitions, and compaction strategies that re-append files, tool artifacts, and images into the running context. The heaviest harness in the benchmark used **5 million tokens where a minimal one used 172 thousand** — and the minimal one’s only tool was a shell.

This maps directly onto a shift visible in what developers are starring. `jcode` — described plainly as *“the most RAM efficient harness,”* Rust, MIT — is at **15,003 stars** since January. Efficiency as a headline feature, not a footnote. The market is pricing leanness.

What makes this actionable for a one-person company: **you can change your harness this week.** You cannot change model pricing, you cannot negotiate an enterprise rate, and you probably cannot fine-tune your way out of a cost problem. But you can run your own ten representative tasks through three harnesses and pick the one that finishes them for the fewest effective tokens. That is a half-day of work against a multiple-x recurring saving.

### 2. Cost observability is being withdrawn exactly when it matters most

Removing dollar figures from a usage dashboard is a small product decision with an outsized signal. The reaction was immediate and told you where the market’s trust level sits: *“There’s no way to attempt to brand this as anything but negative for the user, and positive for the company.”*

The predictable second-order effect showed up in the same thread — a developer already building usage-to-dollar translation for Claude Code and Codex noted he would now be adding Cursor support. **When a vendor removes a number, someone else sells it back.**

Note what this means structurally. Per-token prices are published and comparable. Effective cost per completed task is not, because it depends on harness overhead, cache hit rate, compaction frequency, and retry behavior — none of which appear on a pricing page. As vendors get less forthcoming about the observable half, the gap between *listed price* and *what you actually pay* becomes the thing worth measuring, and the thing worth selling.

There is also a churn signal here that a solo builder should read carefully. Cursor’s original advantage was frictionless migration from VS Code. As one commenter put it, that door swings both ways. **Any moat built on ease-of-adoption is symmetrically an ease-of-departure.** If your own product’s retention story is “it was easy to start,” you do not have a retention story.

### 3. Routing lost the cost argument to caching, and the market noticed in public

A YC S26 company launched a gateway that routes agent traffic turn-by-turn between models — matching frontier performance at roughly half the cost, by their measurement. The technically interesting part is that their router is explicitly cache-aware: switching models only when the cache is cold, because switching a hot cache destroys the economics.

The top comment dismantled the addressable window with one observation: hot cache reads cut input cost by around 90%, and modern agentic work is **long strings of successive tool calls that keep the cache hot throughout**. Cold-cache moments occur mainly when a human replies after a gap long enough for the cache to expire. Those moments are now the rare case, not the common one.

This is the second month running that the routing thesis has been publicly walked back toward caching. The practical conclusion for a solopreneur is unchanged and worth repeating because it is so unglamorous: **prefix stability beats cleverness.** Put everything stable at the front of the prompt, stop invalidating it every turn, keep prefixes above the 1,024-token activation minimum, and you will capture more savings than any routing layer will hand you — with no new dependency and no new vendor.

### 4. Free-tier aggregation has become real infrastructure, with real terms-of-service risk

**OmniRoute** — MIT, **37,221 stars, 4,822 forks**, started in February, built by a claimed 500+ contributors — aggregates **290+ providers and 90+ free tiers** behind one endpoint, advertises roughly **1.53B free tokens per month**, and ships prompt-compression claiming 15–95% token reduction. It works as a drop-in for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Cline, and Copilot.

Give the project credit for the disclosure most aggregators skip: it publishes its dedupe methodology, states that figures are re-audited every two weeks and **“move both ways,”** and explicitly flags **15 providers as ToS-questionable** so the user decides. That is more honest than most commercial dashboards.

Use it with clear eyes anyway. A free tier is a marketing budget, and marketing budgets end. Building unit economics on stacked free tiers means your margin is a coupon that 290 different companies can each revoke without notice. **Excellent for prototyping and for the zero-revenue phase; disqualifying as the cost structure in a pitch or an acquisition conversation.**

### 5. Generation quality crossed a production threshold in video, and the agent-browser gap is closing

Two capability moves this week are worth tracking because they change what one person can deliver.

**Seedance 2.5** (July 31) generates **30 seconds of audio-video in a single pass**, with multi-round extension to multi-minute output that holds character, environment, and pacing consistency. It accepts **up to 30 images, 10 video clips, and 10 audio clips as references in one pass**, and supports timestamp-level editing, green screen, and camera-perspective control. Rolling out on Jimeng and Doubao Pro, with API access via BytePlus ModelArk announced as coming.

Read past the demo. The operationally significant part is not length, it is **consistency across extensions plus timestamp-level editing** — that is the difference between generating clips and revising a cut. When you can fix second 14 without regenerating the whole thing, one person can run an iterative video pipeline. Note the API is not shipped yet; plan around console access for now.

Separately, **ego-lite** (MIT, **7,443 stars** since April) attacks a smaller but nastier problem: agents and humans fighting over the same browser. It gives agents isolated Spaces that reuse your real logged-in session while your tabs stay yours. Its own framing of the incumbent problem is accurate — conventional automation frameworks *“need a separate browser to drive, logins never carry cleanly.”* Login state has been the silent tax on every browser-agent product; this is a credible attempt to remove it.

## Build Directions

### Direction 1 — Effective-cost observability for agent teams

**What to build:** the number vendors are removing. Translate token usage into dollars per completed task, broken out by harness, model, cache-hit rate, and compaction events. Not a pretty dashboard — a per-task cost attribution ledger.

**Stack:** a proxy in front of provider APIs (or parse harness logs directly for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode); provider usage APIs for reconciliation; SQLite or Postgres plus a small Next.js frontend; ship a CLI first because that is what the buyers live in. Build with a lean harness so you feel your own cost surface.

**Buyer and price:** engineering teams of 5–50 whose AI line item became visible to finance this quarter. **$29–99/month per seat**, or **$300–800/month** at the org level. Build time: **2–3 weeks** to a usable v1.

**Why now:** a major vendor just deleted this data, the complaint thread hit the front page, and a developer publicly announced he is adding support for exactly this. Demand is not hypothetical — it is in the comments.

### Direction 2 — Harness fit audit as a productized service

**What to build:** a fixed-scope engagement. Take a client’s ten to twenty representative tasks, run them across three or four harnesses on their real repository, and report effective token cost, completion rate, and compaction frequency for each. Deliver a written recommendation, not a subscription.

**Stack:** a candidate set spanning the weight spectrum — a minimal shell-only harness, a mid-weight one like OpenCode or Pi, and a heavy one like Codex or Claude Code; a runner script; measure uncached tokens, cached tokens, output tokens, and pass rate together. Report effective input as `uncached + 0.1 × cached`, and always report completion rate alongside — a cheap harness that fails long tasks is not cheap.

**Value math:** a team spending $6,000/month on agent inference sitting on a mid-weight default could plausibly see a 2–3x effective reduction on the short-task portion of their mix. Price at **$3,000–6,000 per engagement**, delivered in **4–6 days**, repeatable quarterly as harnesses change.

**Why now:** the benchmark that makes this legible was published this week and is circulating. You are selling a measurement people just learned they should have.

### Direction 3 — Prefix engineering and cache-rate remediation

**What to build:** a narrow technical service that raises cache hit rate. Restructure system prompts and conversation handling so the stable portion sits at the front and stops being invalidated; ensure prefixes clear the **1,024-token activation minimum**; reduce compaction frequency by trimming what gets re-appended.

**Stack:** provider usage APIs and gateway logs for baseline measurement; a rewrite pass on prompt assembly; before/after measurement on the same task set. The whole method is boring and that is the selling point.

**Value math:** the observable range runs from 6% to 91% cache share. Moving a team from 40% to 85% with reads at ~90% off takes a meaningful bite out of the largest line item. Price at **$1,500–4,000**, delivered in **3–5 days**.

**Why now:** two consecutive public retreats from the routing thesis have left teams knowing routing is not the answer without knowing what is. This is the answer, and almost nobody is packaging it.

### Direction 4 — Vertical agents built deliberately lean

**What to build:** a single-job agent for a specific buyer — contract clause review, changelog generation, support triage, invoice reconciliation — implemented on a minimal harness rather than a general-purpose one, with the context budget as an explicit design constraint.

**Stack:** a shell-tool-only or thin custom harness; your domain logic as the system prompt’s stable prefix (which is also your cache anchor, so structure it once and never churn it); a mid-tier model rather than frontier, because narrow scope tolerates it; Supabase or Postgres for state.

**Why this wins:** a general harness carries the cost of generality on every single call. In a narrow vertical you know the task shape in advance, so the overhead a heavy harness pays for — broad tool discovery, expansive skills files, defensive compaction — buys you nothing. **Your competitor running the same model on a stock heavy harness has a structurally worse cost basis than you do, permanently.** That is a margin moat available to a single person, and it does not require a better model.

**Price:** **$99–499/month** per customer depending on volume. Build time: **3–5 weeks** for a defensible v1.

### Direction 5 — Domain knowledge packaged as agent skills

**What to build:** curated, verified skill packs for a specific profession. The mechanic is proven — `book-to-skill` (MIT, **14,974 stars** since May) converts technical book PDFs into Claude Code skills. The open opportunity is not the converter, it is **the curated, rights-cleared, professionally verified pack.**

**Stack:** the conversion pipeline is commodity and open source; your work is source selection, licensing, structuring for retrieval, and expert review. Distribute as versioned repos or a subscription that ships updates when the underlying standards change.

**Buyer and price:** professionals whose domain changes on a schedule — tax, building codes, medical coding, aviation regs, regional compliance. **$19–79/month** individual, more for team licenses.

**Why now:** fifteen thousand stars on the converter proves demand for the format and simultaneously proves the converter itself is not the business. Be careful and explicit about copyright — converting a book you bought for personal use is one thing; selling packs derived from it is another. Build on standards, public regulations, and licensed material.

## Risk Factors

⚠️ **Do not over-correct to the smallest harness.** The 29x headline is 6.7x once cached tokens are priced, and heavy harnesses carry context deliberately to survive long-horizon tasks. Benchmarking on ten short tasks and migrating your whole workflow is how you save 60% on tokens and lose 20% of completion rate. Measure completion alongside cost, always.

⚠️ **Free tiers are a coupon, not a cost structure.** A gateway aggregating 90+ free tiers is superb for prototyping and indefensible as unit economics. The project itself flags 15 providers as ToS-questionable and re-audits its numbers every two weeks because they move both ways. Know which of your margin is real.

⚠️ **Cost opacity will spread.** One vendor removing dollar figures is a data point; it is also a template that works. Assume the trend continues and instrument your own spend at the proxy layer, where nobody can take it away from you.

⚠️ **Ease of adoption is symmetric.** The frictionless onboarding that grows you cuts equally on the way out. If your product’s stickiness rests on setup convenience rather than accumulated data, workflow depth, or genuine switching cost, you are one pricing misstep from a churn event.

⚠️ **Video API access lags the demo.** Seedance 2.5 is live in consumer surfaces with API access still announced rather than shipped. Do not sell a pipeline you cannot yet automate.

⚠️ **AI advice quality is uneven in ways users cannot detect.** MIT Sloan researchers found LLM financial advice is broadly sound on saving and diversification, yet **fails to adjust to shocks like unemployment and lets portfolios drift instead of rebalancing** — with advice varying by the user’s gender, financial literacy, and familiarity with prompting. If you build advisory products in any regulated domain, the failure mode is not obvious nonsense; it is competent-sounding advice that quietly ignores the user’s actual situation. Test on edge cases and shocks, not happy paths.

## Action Recommendations

**If you are starting out (0–1):**

- ✅ Before writing product code, run five representative tasks through two harnesses and record token counts. You will learn more about your future unit economics in one afternoon than from a month of pricing-page research.
- ✅ Default to a lean harness for anything narrow and well-defined. Reserve heavy harnesses for genuinely long-horizon work.
- ✅ Structure your system prompt as a stable prefix from day one, and keep it above 1,024 tokens so caching actually activates. Retrofitting prefix stability into a shipped product is painful.
- ✅ Use free-tier aggregation to prototype at zero cost — then model your real economics at paid rates before you price anything.

**If you already have something running (1–n):**

- ✅ Instrument dollars per completed task this week, at your own proxy layer. If your only cost visibility is a vendor dashboard, you have already seen what happens to those.
- ✅ Compute your actual cache share of input tokens. Under 70% and prefix restructuring is your highest-return engineering task this month, ahead of any feature.
- ✅ Re-run your harness choice against your current task mix. If you picked it six months ago, you picked it on different evidence and probably a different task profile.
- ✅ Kill any routing project. Two public post-mortems now agree that cache economics eat the routing thesis in agentic workloads. Redirect that effort to prefix stability.
- ✅ Audit your own churn symmetry: write down honestly why a customer could not leave next month. If the answer is “it would be mildly annoying,” fix that before shipping another feature.

## What it means

The industry spent two years optimizing the model layer because that is where the price list is. The measurement that landed this week says the wrapper around the model swings your bill by roughly 6.7x after honest cache accounting — and unlike model pricing, that variable is entirely yours to set.

That is the whole opportunity. Model quality is converging and model pricing is set by companies with more leverage than you. Harness overhead, cache discipline, and context budget are engineering decisions a single person can make on a Tuesday, and they compound into a permanent margin advantage over every competitor running the same model on a stock configuration.

One vendor just made its costs harder to see. The correct response is not to complain about the dashboard. It is to build your own measurement, at your own layer, where it cannot be removed — and then to notice that the same instrumentation is a product other people will pay for.

Measure the wrapper, not just the model. The bill was never really about the tokens per unit.
