**What this means for you:** stop building a general assistant. Pick one role in one industry that a real business pays a real salary for today, and build the agent that does *that job* end to end.

### 2. Voice is where the money is actually landing

For two years voice AI was impressive and unprofitable. This week it flipped. FONIO.AI’s raise — and the broader rush of voice-agent infrastructure aimed at SMBs — signals voice crossing into recurring revenue.

The logic is brutally simple. The phone is the one channel a plumber, a dental clinic, a law-firm front desk, or a repair shop *cannot* abandon, and the one they least want to staff. An agent that answers 24/7, screens intent, and books straight into the calendar has a value the owner can compute in one sentence. The defensibility, again, isn’t the model. It’s the **industry-specific call script plus the integration into that vertical’s scheduling/CRM/order system** — precisely the depth a solopreneur can build and a horizontal platform won’t bother to.

**What this means for you:** if you’re going to build one thing this month, a voice agent for a narrow, phone-dependent trade is the highest-signal bet on the board right now.

### 3. Distribution is shifting from SEO to answer engines (AEO/GEO)

If you get customers through content and organic traffic, there’s a structural change you can’t ignore. People increasingly ask an AI — ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Google’s AI Overviews — “which tool does X?” and take the synthesized answer instead of clicking ten links. A real chunk of top-of-funnel search traffic is being intercepted.

- **Old game:** keyword density and backlinks to win the first page of results.
- **New game (AEO/GEO):** get your product and content *cited inside the answer the AI gives*. Structured comparison pages, clean “X vs Y” breakdowns, and data-backed, bylined first-hand tests are what these engines prefer to quote.

**What this means for you:** replace the generic “top 10 AI tools” listicle with structured, citable depth — a clear question, a clear answer, real numbers, real hands-on. That single asset earns classic search *and* AI-answer citations.

## Build Directions

### 1. A voice agent for one boring, phone-dependent trade

**What to build:** pick a narrow trade where the phone is the lifeline and nobody wants to answer it — dental clinics, auto repair, home services, law-firm reception, real-estate. ### 2. A “shift-lead” agent for frontline operations

**What to build:** ORBIO’s shape, narrowed. Pick one operational scene — restaurant scheduling, retail store checks, outsourced-team delivery tracking — and build an agent that tracks progress, sends reminders, auto-compiles the daily report, and escalates exceptions to the owner. ### 3. Package a vertical workflow as an installable skill / MCP server

**What to build:** don’t ship a full app — ship a capability another agent can call. Wrap one vertical’s tool calls, data connectors, prompt scaffolding and guardrails as a skill or MCP server. Let the marketplace carry distribution.

**Stack:** an MCP server in `Python/TypeScript` around your domain logic; a skill manifest and a small eval set; publish to LarryBrain / OpenClaw skills / opc.dev and any platform MCP registry in your vertical.

**Cost/timeline:** 2–5 weeks to a listable skill. $5–9 one-time for simple ones; subscription if there’s ongoing API cost.

### 4. “Get-cited-by-AI” content service for indie builders (AEO)

**What to build:** a done-for-you service that makes a product show up inside AI answers — structured comparison pages, FAQ schema, citable hands-on tests, and presence in the communities and directories these engines read. The customer base is every builder who just realized their product is invisible to search *and* to ChatGPT.

**Stack:** an LLM to batch-draft comparisons/FAQs + structured schema markup + Perplexity/ChatGPT monitoring to check “did my product get cited?”

**Cost/timeline:** 1–3 weeks to first output. $500–$2k per project or $200–$600/month for ongoing monitoring and tuning.

## Risk Factors

- **Voice agent failures are expensive.** It answers real calls and books real appointments — one recognition error is a real business loss. Ship a fallback script and a human-handoff path before you go live. Don’t run it bare.
- **Deep verticals demand real know-how.** A dental-clinic phone flow is nothing like an auto shop’s. Without time in the trade, you’ll build a demo that dies on contact with reality. Co-build with one real customer first.
- **Marketplace and platform risk is real.** Skill stores and platform MCP surfaces are distribution *and* a chokepoint — they set the take rate and the rules, and can change both. Don’t make one marketplace your only channel.
- **AEO is a moving target.** Answer-engine crawling and citation rules are unstandardized and shifting weekly. Treat it as ongoing maintenance, not a one-time fix.
