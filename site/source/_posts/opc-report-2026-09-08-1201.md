# Your Customers Are Building Your Product Themselves — Sell Workflow Redesign, Not Tools

## September 8, 2026 | Daily Monitoring

> **TL;DR:** Software procurement flipped from *buy* to *build*. Roughly a third of organizations have already killed a purchase because a coding agent can produce it internally — which removes the floor under generic horizontal micro-SaaS. But the same research exposes the counter-opportunity: 80% of people report AI made them personally more productive, only 37% of organizations can attribute any profit to it, and just 6% see significant returns. The gap isn’t models or tools. It’s that almost nobody redesigned the workflow. That gap is a service you can sell this month.

---

## Key Insights

The most useful thing published this cycle isn’t a model release. It’s a measurement of where AI value leaks out.

McKinsey’s 2026 State of AI survey (1,719 respondents across 97 countries, fielded May 4 – June 8) introduced a metric that didn’t exist in prior years: **32% of organizations have decided against buying at least one software product or feature because they can now build it internally with agentic coding tools.** In technology that figure is 41%; in healthcare, 39%.

Read alongside the ROI numbers, it produces a clear strategic instruction. Adoption is near-universal (89% use AI in at least one function, 44% scaling enterprise-wide), individual benefit is real and widely felt (80%), and organizational financial impact has not moved for a full year (37% attributing any EBIT impact, 6% calling it significant — both flat).

**Numbers that should change what you build this week:**

- **32%** of organizations cancelled a software purchase because coding agents can build it in-house (**41%** in tech, **39%** in healthcare).
- **74%** of AI “high performers” fundamentally redesigned workflows, versus roughly **25%** of everyone else — a ~50-point gap that is pure addressable service demand.
- Large enterprises (>$1B revenue) scaling agents rose **27% → 40%** in a year; smaller organizations stayed **flat at 22%**. The underserved market is the small end.
- **20%** of organizations say AI operating costs — including token costs — are already constraining their usage.

## Market Trends

### 1. “Build” Is Eating the Generic Software Budget

This is the line that should make every indie developer re-audit their roadmap. A third of buyers now have an internal alternative that didn’t exist eighteen months ago: an engineer with a coding agent that doesn’t sleep.

- Any horizontal feature that a competent developer plus an agent can reproduce in a weekend has no moat. It’s no longer a question of whether your product is good — it’s whether it’s *cheaper to buy than to generate*.
- The products surviving this shift share three traits: **data the customer can’t obtain**, **compliance or audit burden that makes in-house builds uneconomical**, and **integration networks that are tedious to replicate** (five legacy systems, not one clean API).
- Independent-developer data points the same direction. Roughly **68%** of profitable one-person SaaS businesses serve a vertical niche — contractors, Shopify sellers, clinics — rather than a broad market. Median time to first paying customer: **6 weeks** for vertical products versus **4+ months** for horizontal tools.

**Meaning:** your selection criterion changed. The old question was “does anyone want this feature?” The new question is “could my customer’s coding agent produce this by lunchtime?” If yes, don’t build it.

### 2. The 80 / 37 / 6 Fracture Is Itself the Business

Three numbers from one survey, read together:

| Metric | Value | Year-over-year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report AI improved their **personal** productivity | 80% | — |
| Organizations attributing **any** EBIT impact to AI | 37% | flat |
| “High performers” (≥5% of EBIT from AI, significant impact) | 6% | flat |

The industry spent another year of budget and the organizational needle didn’t move. Where does the value leak?

- **Into unchanged processes.** 74% of high performers redesigned the underlying workflow; only ~25% of the rest did. Most companies bolted a chat window onto an existing process — the old machine runs faster, the P&L reads the same.
- **Into individuals.** Time an employee saves returns to that employee by default. It becomes slack, or gets absorbed by work that expands to fill it. Converting it to profit requires someone to change task routing, staffing, or pricing — and that’s in nobody’s job description.
- **Confirmed at the source.** OpenAI’s own usage split makes the point: 98% of internal staff use Codex, but under 1% of individual external subscribers and only 17% of enterprise subscribers use agent features. The gap between tool capability and organizational capability is wide.
- **The labor-substitution story underdelivered.** In 2025, 32% of respondents expected AI-driven headcount reductions within a year. Actual: **14%**. Cost stories built on replacing people are converting at under half the projected rate.

**Reframe it as a market:** the 31 points between 37% and 6% are companies that already bought tools, already feel individual gains, and *cannot produce financial evidence*. They don’t need another tool. They need someone to take the process apart, reassemble it, and hand them one defensible number. That is the shortest sales conversation available to a solo founder right now.

### 3. Agent Security Review Hardened Into a Permanent Gate

Over the past week the security industry collectively decided that vetting agents is a standing product category, not a stopgap. The practical consequence for an independent builder: **when you deliver an agent to an enterprise, there is now a firewall in front of its tools and a runtime monitor watching what it does.**

- **AIR** came out of stealth with a **$50M** seed co-led by Sequoia and Greenoaks to build an inline “firewall for AI agents” — continuously discovering and vetting every skill, plugin, and MCP server an organization’s agents touch. It reports filtering roughly **27%** of agent add-ons as unsafe across 20+ customers, mostly finance and pharma. Its research found **17,800+** public AI add-ons (6.7M installs) relying on untrusted external instruction sources, including Skills impersonating Anthropic and OpenAI to bypass review and execute arbitrary code.
- One day later **HiddenLayer** raised a **$100M** Series B for runtime protection, reporting 10x ARR growth in twelve months across 50+ new customers in banking, insurance, pharma, airlines, and US defense — and shipped Agent Harness Security aimed specifically at coding agents.
- **CrowdStrike** launched an AI Partner Specialization with a “Verified Agent” certification; **Tenable** shipped AI Inspector to vet third-party agents pre-deployment; **Proofpoint** delivered a SOC Analyst Agent. **Okta** raised full-year guidance and explicitly credited demand for agent identity and access management.
- The risk side has receipts. Manifold Security disclosed **GitSpawn**: 8 vulnerabilities across 7 coding agents — including Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and Goose — exploiting Git’s `core.fsmonitor` setting to trigger code execution via malicious `.git` configs *before a user approves anything*. **Four of eight were unpatched at disclosure.**
- Capital is stacking in the same layer: roughly **20 disclosed agent rounds totaling ~$1.32B** in Q3 to date, with average check size jumping from **~$49M in July to ~$97M in August** while deal count held steady. Bigger bets, concentrated in infrastructure and governance.

**Meaning:** wire scoped identity, a tool allow-list, an audit trail, and a kill switch into version one. Not for polish — without those four, your first serious pilot dies in a security review rather than on features.

## Build Directions

### Direction 1 · Productize Workflow Redesign (top pick this cycle)

- **What:** Attack the 31-point gap directly. Pick one concrete function — support tickets, accounts payable, lead qualification, claims intake — and deliver three stages: **diagnose** (baseline cycle time and cost per unit), **redesign** (change task routing and human checkpoints, not add a chat box), **measure** (hand over one defensible number: cost per ticket, cycle time, contractor spend that didn’t renew). You’re selling provable P&L evidence, not software.
- **Stack:** n8n or LangGraph for orchestration (so you can *show* which step changed) + Claude Code / Codex for glue code + PostHog or direct DB queries for before/after baselines + a two-page redesign memo as the artifact.
- **Cost / timeline:** tools ~$150–300/mo. Diagnosis 1–2 weeks — **charge $3,000–8,000 for it separately**, which also filters out unserious buyers. Full delivery 4–6 weeks, then $2,000–5,000/mo retainer.
- **Why now:** the buyer already owns tools and already feels individual gains but can’t show finance anything. Shortest possible persuasion path — zero market education required.

### Direction 2 · “Good Enough” Agent Security for SMBs

- **What:** Large enterprises can buy AIR, HiddenLayer, and Tenable. Small businesses can’t afford or operate them — one reason SMB agent deployment has been **stuck at 22% for a year**. Ship the lightweight version: inventory of what each agent may call, version pinning and provenance checks for third-party skills and MCP servers, human confirmation on sensitive actions, and one exportable audit log. Position it as the checkpoint an agent must clear before going live.
- **Stack:** open-source governance components (authorization gate + audit chain) + Supabase for audit records and permissions + Claude Code for integration + Stripe. Ship “a script plus a report” first; do not start with a console.
- **Cost / timeline:** open-source free, Supabase Free–$25/mo + coding agent $20–100/mo; 3–4 weeks to a usable version. Price $300–1,500/mo, or $2,000–5,000 per audit.
- **Opening line:** cite GitSpawn (7 coding agents, 8 flaws, 4 unpatched at disclosure) and AIR’s 27% unsafe-add-on rate. Those two facts outsell any pitch deck.

### Direction 3 · Vertical Micro-SaaS Where In-House Building Doesn’t Pay

- **What:** Since 32% of purchases have shifted to build, only ship what’s uneconomical to build: **compliance-bearing** (HIPAA records, industry filings, state-specific tax rules), **integration-heavy** (five legacy systems at once), or **data you have and they don’t**. Live shapes worth studying: contractor invoice automation ($49–99/mo), solo-therapist scheduling plus compliant notes ($79/mo), clinic vaccine inventory and expiry tracking (25 clinics ≈ $2K MRR).
- **Stack:** Next.js + Supabase (Postgres/Auth/Storage — **enable Row Level Security on day one**) + Stripe Billing + Resend + Vercel; build with Claude Code or Cursor.
- **Cost / timeline:** $50–200/mo all-in (free tiers carry you to ~1,000 users); MVP 2–4 weeks; median 6 weeks to first paying customer for vertical products.
- **Validation:** before building, find 10 people in the niche, describe it in one sentence, ask “if this were $49/month, would you pay starting next week?” Fewer than 3 yes — change the idea.

### Direction 4 · FinOps for Agents (Cost Attribution and Unit Economics)

- **What:** 20% of organizations are already cost-constrained, and almost none know which step is burning the money. Build per-agent / per-workflow / per-customer attribution of token and tool-call spend, plus redundant-call detection and cache-hit optimization. The value proposition is unambiguous: cut the bill, take a cut of the savings.
- **Stack:** OpenTelemetry/OTLP trace collection + Postgres or ClickHouse for detail + a minimal Next.js dashboard + Stripe. Anchor the cost model to live pricing — e.g. Claude Fable 5.1 at $10 in / $50 out per million tokens with **cache reads down to $0.25 (a 75% cut)**, which makes caching strategy a quantifiable savings line by itself.
- **Cost / timeline:** infra $30–80/mo; 2–3 weeks to public beta; price $99–499/mo, or 15–25% of demonstrated savings (the second closes faster).

### Direction 5 · “Pass the Security Review” as a Solo Delivery Service

- **What:** Plenty of teams have a working agent that’s stalled in an enterprise security review. Productize clearing that gate: scoped credentials, tool allow-lists, structured logging, a kill switch, plus a document the client can hand straight to their security team. You’re packaging the demo-to-production gap as a fixed deliverable.
- **Stack:** identity and permissions (scoped credentials + allow-list) + structured logging (OTLP) + explicit human-approval nodes in n8n or LangGraph + a 5–8 page security memo template — **that template is the real reusable asset**.
- **Cost / timeline:** tools $100–200/mo; 2–3 weeks per engagement; price $5,000–15,000 per project or convert to a $2,000–5,000/mo retainer. By the third engagement the template halves your delivery time.

## Risk Factors

- **Your customer is building your product internally.** 32% have already cancelled a software purchase (41% in tech). Any horizontal feature reproducible in a weekend has no defense — pressure-test every idea against “could their agent do this by lunchtime?”
- **Security review is the new failure point.** Enterprise buyers now sit behind agent firewalls and runtime monitors. Without scoped identity, tool allow-lists, audit trails, and a kill switch, your first real pilot stalls in review rather than on capability.
- **Your own toolchain is an attack surface.** GitSpawn spans 8 flaws across 7 mainstream coding agents, four unpatched at disclosure, executing code before any user approval. Pin dependency versions, verify provenance, and stop installing unvetted skills and MCP servers.
- **Compute cost eats margin quietly.** 20% of organizations are already constrained by AI operating costs. Price token spend into your quote with a buffer, especially for high-intensity agentic workloads. Run the unit economics before you set the number.
- **Foundation-model legal exposure flows downstream.** Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell have sued Anthropic over training data, with no damages figure specified yet. If your product depends on a single model vendor, keep a switchable fallback path ready.

## Action Recommendations

**For starters (0 → 1):**

- ✅ Re-run your idea list through one filter this week: “could their coding agent build this by lunchtime?” Delete every yes. Keep the ones carrying compliance burden, integration burden, or proprietary data.
- ✅ Don’t start with a product — sell a **paid diagnosis** first ($3,000–8,000, 1–2 weeks). It validates demand, yields real process data, and screens out tire-kickers.
- ✅ Ask 10 people in the target niche “would you pay $49/month starting next week?” before writing code. Fewer than 3 yes, change the idea. Far cheaper than building.
- ✅ Wire scoped identity + tool allow-list + one audit log line into v1. Two days of work; saves rebuilding the entire governance layer later.

**For mature teams (product already live):**

- ✅ Assemble a **security packet** (permission model, log samples, kill-switch mechanism, data-flow diagram). Highest ROI catch-up work available right now for enterprise win rates.
- ✅ Compute your customer’s ROI *for* them and ship it with delivery: before/after cost per unit or cycle time. In an environment where 37% can’t produce financial evidence, a defensible number is your pricing premium.
- ✅ Audit your own unit economics immediately: token plus tool-call cost per customer against revenue per customer. One in five peers is already cost-constrained.
- ✅ Treat SMBs as the primary front, not the discount tier. Large-enterprise agent scaling went 27% → 40% in a year while smaller organizations sat at 22%. That 18-point delta is unserved demand.

## What It Means

The cheap era of shipping a tool is closing, and it’s closing from the demand side rather than the supply side. When a third of your prospective buyers can generate your feature internally, “we built a thing that works” stops being a business. What survives the shift is what agents can’t manufacture on their own: knowledge of a specific industry’s mess, data nobody else holds, the tedious compliance and integration work that makes in-house builds uneconomical — and the organizational change nobody has an incentive to do.

That last one is the underpriced asset. Eighty percent of people feel faster while ninety-four percent of companies can’t prove it paid. The bottleneck is no longer capability; it’s conversion. Sell the conversion. Build the wrench that turns individual speed into a line on the income statement — and make sure it clears the security gate on the way in.
