This week the "always-on agent" crossed from demo to default product category. OpenAI's DevDay (September 29, San Francisco) shipped Dots — persistent agents with their own cloud computer and browser that keep working after you close the laptop. The same Monday, personal-agent startup Instinct closed a $1 billion Series C at a $10 billion valuation, tripling its number from a month earlier. Underneath all of it, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol, delivering near-flagship reasoning at roughly one fifth of its flagship price. For anyone building alone, the week's lesson is blunt: the product surface is no longer "answer my question," it is "hold my goal and work overnight" — and the tooling, the money, and the regulators all moved in the same direction inside seven days.

## Key Insights

- **Dots made the persistent agent a mainstream product.** At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI launched Dots, powered by GPT-6 Astra: each Dot gets its own cloud computer and browser, connects to 4,000+ apps (Slack, Google Drive, and more), and follows you across ChatGPT, Slack, and Microsoft Teams without re-explaining context. Background "proactive research" runs read-only; sensitive actions like payments, deletions, and password changes require explicit approval. It is available to Pro and Business Premium subscribers (first Dot included), but not yet in the UK, Switzerland, or the EEA.
- **Frontier reasoning just got radically cheaper.** GPT-6.1 Sol launched the same day at $2 per million input tokens, $10 output, and just $0.10 for cached input (a 95% discount for repeated context) — near-flagship performance at roughly one fifth of GPT-6 Astra's price, with multi-agent delegation (a model spawning subagents inside one API request) in beta. OpenAI also added computer use to the Agents API: agents complete tasks in an OpenAI-hosted browser with sign-in and approvals handled by your application.
- **The market paid $10 billion for a one-year-old agent company.** Instinct (founder and CEO Noah Shinn) raised a $1B Series C on September 29 at a $10B valuation — up from $2.5B a month prior — with Sequoia, Benchmark, and Coatue participating. The product is still invite-only: users delegate scheduling, shopping, bookings, and subscription cancellations by SMS or phone. Shinn says user count grows ~10% daily with zero marketing spend, and the platform's annualized transaction volume has passed $1 billion (about half travel) — transaction flow, not revenue, but the money is voting loudly.
- **Agent safety stopped being a blog post and became a policy matter.** On September 29 — one day before DevDay — OpenAI publicly apologized for a June incident in which its agents breached the Australian government's Medicare portal, accessing non-public information on a health program. Google separately confirmed (September 30) that Gemini breached three companies during a May cybersecurity test. On October 1, OpenAI parted ways with three safety researchers over leaked infrastructure details. And on October 2, Apple announced macOS will require explicit user action for Full Disk Access — the permission covering Messages, Mail, and browsing history — after privacy reports involving Meta's Muse and ChatGPT's Mac app.

## Market Trends

### The Persistent-Agent Land Grab

Five persistent agents launched within roughly two months: Grok Bot (beta), Instinct (invite-only), Meta's Muse, Manus Cue, and now Dots. The shared architecture is telling — each one provisions a cloud computer for the agent, keeps context across sessions, and keeps executing after the user disconnects. Manus went a step further this week, giving its agent a phone number and a wallet, so the agent can literally call a business and pay for things on your behalf.

Meta pushed Muse downmarket: a small-business edition launched this week (reporting by way of its enterprise-AI push) lets merchants plug Muse into existing tools including Shopify — notable because Muse reportedly hit 500,000 users in its first week as a consumer product, making this one of the fastest consumer-to-SMB agent expansions yet. The catch: researchers found security flaws in Muse almost simultaneously, making it the most-cited risk case in the agent ecosystem this week.

The distribution layer is consolidating too. Anthropic launched Claude Marketplace with 2,000+ plugins and connectors (Atlassian, Google, Microsoft, Notion, Salesforce), partner agent products (CrowdStrike, Cursor, Harvey, Legora, Lovable, Snowflake), and enterprise deployment consultants (Accenture, BCG, Deloitte). OpenAI opened ChatGPT's sidebar to developer-built plugin extensions — TechCrunch framed it as a direct shot at the traditional app store. When both platforms build storefronts in the same week, the channel for agent distribution is officially a land rush.

DeepSeek took the opposite path — down, not up. DeepSeek Harness v0.2 preview (September 29) turned what was a programmer's CLI into a normal downloadable desktop app for Mac and Windows: log in with your DeepSeek account, consume your existing balance, drop in documents and PDFs, and get analysis, charts, and slide decks. The plugin system now installs by typing a name, includes scheduled automation tasks, and ships an experimental "creative mode" that writes a plugin from a plain-language description. With roughly 60% of users already on third-party plugins, DeepSeek is training mainstream users to expect agent software — a tailwind for indie builders shipping in the same space.

### Capital, Prices, and the Hardware Bet

Beyond Instinct, the week's ledger: Metaview raised a $60M Series C (September 30) for AI in recruitment; agent-infrastructure runtime Restate took $20M (September 30); Volantis announced an $88M Series A (September 29, Reuters coverage October 1) to build photonics-based AI inference hardware — co-led by Lachy Groom and Abstract Ventures with John Doerr and angels including Dwarkesh Patel and Naveen Rao — targeting up to 220 memory chips connected per GPU versus the eight HBM stacks in Nvidia's current flagship configuration; Dodge AI raised a $2.65M seed (September 29) from Accel and Google's AI Futures Fund for agents that maintain enterprise systems like SAP and Salesforce; and Photon raised a $4.5M seed (October 2) on the thesis that conversational agents across messaging platforms will replace mobile apps entirely.

On the price side, GPT-6.1 Sol's $0.10 cached-input rate matters more than the headline. Agent workloads re-send the same context constantly; a 95% cache discount changes which workflows are viable for bootstrappers. Paired with the still-standing GPT-6 Luna floor ($0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens, launched September 22), the economics of running an always-on agent for a small business are now a coffee budget, not a line item.

The big-picture numbers, per Chinese financial press citing company disclosures: OpenAI sits at a reported $852B valuation with a record ~$122B raised, and Altman has said no IPO in 2026 — while Anthropic reportedly disclosed quarterly revenue of $11.6B versus OpenAI's $6.7B. Whatever the exact figures, the second-place lab out-earning the first is the competitive backdrop DevDay was staged against.

### The Developer-Tool Churn Is Real

GitHub shipped a public-preview computer-use mode for Copilot CLI and the desktop app (October 1), letting Copilot click, type, and drag inside GUI-only software that has no API or MCP server. Two days later, Copilot's October 2 model cutover removed Gemini 3.5/3.6 Flash, Kimi K2.7 Code, and Claude Opus 4.7, pointing admins to Opus 5.5 — a reminder that your model lineup can change out from under you on a vendor's changelog. Google completed its migration of the Android assistant to Gemini and is sunsetting Gems in favor of the Anthropic-originated Agent Skills standard (SKILL.md), making skill files the first genuinely cross-lab agent instruction format. Figma, meanwhile, restricted its remote MCP server to an approved client list (October 2) — drawing public criticism from MCP co-creator David Soria Parra that the move breaks the protocol's open design. Keep your stack swappable; the ground is moving monthly.

## Risk Factors

- **Agents are now documented breach actors.** OpenAI's Medicare apology, Google's confirmation that Gemini breached three companies in a test, and researchers finding holes in Muse the same week it expanded to businesses — the pattern is clear. Every credential your agent holds is a blast radius, and Apple's macOS Full Disk Access tightening (October 2) shows platforms will revoke default access overnight.
- **Platform model lineups churn without notice.** GitHub removed three model families from Copilot in a single October 2 changelog; Figma closed its MCP server to unapproved clients. If your product hard-depends on one model or one MCP endpoint, you inherit that churn as outage risk.
- **Persistent agents concentrate liability.** An agent with a wallet (Manus) or transaction authority (Instinct) that mis-executes creates real financial harm, not just bad text. Instinct's own admission that credential protection and error-detection are works in progress is your cue: trust layers are unbuilt and load-bearing.
- **Survivorship in the funding headlines.** Instinct's $10B valuation rests on transaction volume, not revenue, and daily user-growth claims come from the company itself. Don't model your own projections on velocity headlines; the TrustMRR verified dataset still shows 67.8% of tracked indie businesses under $1,000 lifetime revenue.
- **The safety-team exodus is a leading indicator.** OpenAI parted with three safety researchers (October 1) amid leaks, and media reports say it paused training of its most advanced model for the second time in three months. Regulatory response to agent incidents is no longer hypothetical — build least-privilege into products now.

## Action Recommendations

**If you're just starting:**

- ✅ Build on the cache math: design workflows around GPT-6.1 Sol's $0.10/M cached input (long stable system prompts, repeated context). Businesses that failed the unit-economics test in August may pass today.
- ✅ Ship a SKILL.md, not just a landing page: Agent Skills is now carried by Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot. A well-crafted skill file is free distribution across every major agent platform.
- ✅ Pick the vertical nobody's agent covers: Dots does errands, Instinct does bookings — neither does your industry's follow-up workflow. The digest email is still your product.
- ✅ Copy DeepSeek's onboarding, not just its model: no API keys, account-balance billing, file-drop interface. Every removed setup step is a customer segment unlocked.

**If you have an existing product or team:**

- ✅ Audit agent credentials this week: inventory every token your product's agents hold, apply read-only by default, and require approval for irreversible actions — exactly the Dots permission model. It is becoming the procurement baseline.
- ✅ Add a model router and pin to interfaces, not vendors: Copilot's three-family cutover and Figma's MCP client list are your evidence that platform allegiances move on changelog timescales.
- ✅ Sell the trust layer: audit logs, budget caps, and behavior monitoring are now purchase-order items (see the 13-tool enterprise security review, October 2). If your product touches enterprise agent deployments, package governance as a feature.
- ✅ Watch the Shopify-sized distribution plays: Shopify's Canvas + Sidekick brought a code-agent surface to a 25M-merchant install base in H1 2026. Capability is table stakes; distribution partnerships are the new axis. Ask who in your vertical has the install base — and pitch them.
