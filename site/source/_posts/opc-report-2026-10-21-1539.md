October 21 was the day the AI industry's best-guarded internal metric went public. A position paper submitted to arXiv on September 28 — "What if automating AI R&D triggers an intelligence explosion?" (arXiv:2609.36054) — circulated through Chinese tech media with 22 authors attached, among them Turing Award winners Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, reinforcement-learning pioneer Andrew Barto, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, Microsoft's Eric Horvitz and Berkeley's Dawn Song. Its abstract states plainly that "AI systems now write most of the code inside the companies that build them." The same day, the Economic Daily reported that China registered 7.315 million new one-person companies in 2025, up 42.3% year on year, and that "AI one-person companies" are now written into national policy. One story is about production shrinking to a single human; the other is about that human's tools starting to build themselves. Here is what both mean for a one-person company, with named sources and hard numbers.

## Key Insights

- **The RSI position paper puts numbers on the loop.** Hinton, Bengio, Barto and 19 co-authors assess whether automating AI research and development could trigger an "intelligence explosion" in which years of advances are compressed into months or less. The paper is explicitly a position and policy document — no new training runs — but the lab data it compiles is the story. Chinese coverage of the paper (Datawhale via Sina Finance, Oct 21) reports that the share of Anthropic's code submitted by AI systems and merged into the codebase rose from single digits in January 2025 to over 80% by May 2026, and that Anthropic's September 2026 internal measurement found research work completable with only high-level human supervision rose from roughly 1% in March 2026 to 26% in August 2026. OpenAI's September 2026 disclosure put experiments per active researcher at about 1.6x the 2025 average in August (Tencent News, Oct 20).
- **The paper defines the unit being automated: a six-stage research chain.** Hypothesis generation, benchmark experiment design and execution, distributed-cluster scheduling, metric and log analysis, deep failure diagnosis, and algorithm-course correction — then repeat. Today's agents are strongest in the middle of that chain (running, logging, summarizing) and weakest at the ends (choosing what to study, judging whether a result matters).
- **Solo-scale players are already capturing the same gains — without weight updates.** A method called ALTK-Evolve mines behavioral guidelines from an agent's own task trajectories and appends them to the system prompt. On Sierra's τ²-bench customer-service benchmark, a stock gpt-4.1 agent carrying learned guidelines scored 67.5% Pass^4 on retail — first place on the October 12, 2026 leaderboard snapshot, ahead of RAFT-30B-A3B (61.4%), a model fine-tuned specifically on τ²-bench retail data. With gpt-5.5 as both agent and guideline extractor on airline tasks, accuracy rose from 72.5% to 82.5% and Pass^4 from 55.0% to 70.0% (+15 points), topping the frontier field at 66–72%. No weights were updated and no human annotation was used.
- **Spec-driven porting now has public numbers.** Akka tested a specification-based AI porting workflow across 65 open-source projects: 99.3 hours of work consuming 9.41 billion tokens, with 57 of 65 projects improved in lines of code or performance. Structured specifications — declarations, evidence and typed behavior — improved first-pass implementation, while context files that omitted cross-component decisions hurt it. Sonnet averaged 61 minutes per port versus Opus at 120 minutes, but Opus consumed about 40% fewer tokens (Akka blog, via NetEase, Oct 21).
- **China has written the one-person company into national policy.** Per the Economic Daily (Oct 21), 2025 saw 7.315 million new one-person companies registered, up 42.3% year on year. In June 2026, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and six other departments issued a platform-economy action plan (2026–2028) containing the first national-policy mention of "accelerating the cultivation of AI one-person companies (AI OPC)." Local programs followed: Shanghai's Lingang area offers up to three years of free rent and compute subsidies up to 80%, with five OPC communities certified and a legislative innovation workshop held October 20; Suzhou runs a dedicated AI OPC cultivation plan (2025–2028); Beijing's Chaoyang district is standing up a SICN "super-individual network" agency to broker deals for solo founders.
- **New agent surfaces opened — and new agent risks with them.** OpenAI's ChatGPT browser extension shipped Chat and Work modes with @Chrome invocation and per-site permission scoping, free across Chromium browsers (its own Atlas browser was shut down in August). Telegram's Walt app now lets users connect ChatGPT or Claude to trade leveraged perpetual futures from a dedicated, non-withdrawable account with execution off by default — in a market where $19 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated in a single day in October 2025. Sundar Pichai, meanwhile, described Google's unified Gemini agent as a persistent 24/7 cloud service spinning up "a micro VM effectively for every query," powered by the Argon model.

## Market Trends

**Frontier R&D is quietly becoming an agent-operations discipline.** The practical definition circulating with the paper is blunt: the number of researchers needed to deliver a unit of intelligence should not have to scale with compute. Karpathy's published autoresearch loop is the canonical pattern — an agent edits training code, runs a fixed five-minute single-GPU budget against a fixed evaluation, and the change is kept or reverted before the next round, with the human setting research direction and boundaries. The infrastructure consequence is equally concrete: training systems are being rebuilt so agents can operate, observe and self-correct — callable training interfaces, reproducible environments and independent evaluations decide how many such loops one researcher can supervise. For a one-person company, the leverage is real but conditional: it accrues to whoever owns a trustworthy eval, not to whoever owns the most agent seats.

**The competitive layer is moving from weights to prompts, specs and evals.** ALTK-Evolve beat a purpose-fine-tuned model with a prompt; Akka's structured specifications beat ad-hoc context files on 57 of 65 ports; GitHub's Spec Kit is formalizing the same spec-plan-task-implement loop for coding agents. The pattern across all three: the durable asset is the specification and the evaluation harness, both of which are cheap for a solo operator to build and impossible for a frontier lab to copy from you. The caveat is domain dependence — retail wanted the full guideline playbook while airline reliability preferred a compact high-support core — so there is no universal prompt bible to download.

**Institutions are catching up to the one-person company, unevenly.** The Economic Daily's reporting is candid about the gaps. Han Yue, a former Meitu senior AI product manager with no engineering background, shipped six mobile apps in a year using four to five coordinated agents at about ¥800 per month in AI tooling — but product direction, user-need judgment and quality control stayed hers. A three-person team at Huimi Tech used AI for Japan market research, marketing content and influencer screening, topping Japan's TikTok home-textile category within four months with quarterly sales above ¥100 million — while, as lawyer Fu Qiang noted at the Lingang workshop, bookkeeping, client acquisition and deal negotiation were still done by those same three people: "AI can rapidly amplify an individual's production capacity, but it does not automatically deliver asset monetization, market credit or compliance ability." Guo Fang of the Renmin-University/Westlake joint institute frames the depreciation schedule directly: standardized, describable, verifiable execution ability loses value first; scarcity moves to industry understanding, user acquisition, entry into real business processes, accumulated data and client trust.

## Build Directions

- **Stand up a personal autoresearch loop.** The Karpathy pattern — agent edits, fixed-budget run, fixed eval, keep-or-revert — reproduces at solo scale on any recurring task: prompt variants, pricing copy, onboarding flows, ad creative. The human sets the objective and the evaluation; the agent runs the iterations and never touches the revert decision.
- **Mine your own trajectories into guidelines.** ALTK-Evolve needs no training budget: log agent successes and failures, extract rules with support counts, append the high-support core to the system prompt. Budget for the overhead — retail input tokens rose about 65% ($0.084 to $0.139 per conversation) — and reclaim it with prompt caching, since the guideline block is identical across calls.
- **Port and migrate spec-first.** Akka's numbers argue for structured specifications (declarations, evidence, typed behavior) before any code generation, and for picking models by task shape: Opus for token-heavy complex ports (120 minutes, about 40% fewer tokens), Sonnet for volume and speed.
- **Treat browsers and chat apps as agent surfaces — with scoped permissions.** The ChatGPT extension's per-site scoping and Walt's default-off execution are the emerging safety pattern for agent products; copy both in anything you ship, including a one-tap kill switch.
- **Harvest the policy wave if you operate in China.** Compute subsidies, free rent and SICN-style brokerage are real but time-boxed resources — and the Lingang workshop agenda (monetization, credit, compliance) doubles as a checklist of gaps you must close yourself.

## Risk Factors

- **The headline numbers are self-reported.** Anthropic's 26% is an internal measurement of work "completable with high-level supervision," not autonomous research, and every lab compiling these figures has an incentive to narrate progress. Treat the direction as signal and the levels as marketing.
- **Verification is the bottleneck, not generation.** Analyses accompanying the paper's Chinese coverage note that agents still cannot judge whether a research question is worth asking: in one documented test, a model debugging an mAP drop suggested generic fixes (raise the learning rate, add augmentation) while missing a neck-module feature-alignment design flaw. Your evaluation set is now the product.
- **Guidelines can overfit their own mine.** ALTK-Evolve's rules are extracted from the agent's own trajectories, and the optimum playbook size varied by domain. Re-mine and re-measure per vertical; a single global prompt bible will silently decay.
- **Agentic trading can still wipe the account.** Walt's agents cannot withdraw funds, but nothing stops a bad position from liquidating the dedicated balance; October 2025's single-day $19 billion liquidation is the tail-risk reference. Default-off execution is a courtesy, not a control.
- **Policy support is local and expiring.** Three-year rent holidays and 80% compute subsidies end on schedule, and IP enforcement for AI-generated content remains slow — one founder described buying copyright registrations he could not himself interpret.

## Action Recommendations

1. **This week:** instrument one closed loop on a real task — fixed evaluation, keep/revert logging, a five-minute-equivalent budget — and count how many iterations complete without you in the loop.
2. **This month:** extract guidelines from your own agent logs with support counts, A/B them against current prompts, and track Pass^k-style reliability rather than single-run wins.
3. **Before year-end:** run one port or migration spec-first (declarations, evidence, typed behavior) and log tokens and hours against the 57-of-65 improved benchmark class.
4. **Watch items:** the policy proposals attached to arXiv:2609.36054 (visibility into R&D automation, steering and constraint mechanisms); Anthropic's next internal measurement; Lingang's legislative outcomes; any expansion of Walt-style agent trading into regulated venues.

**Bottom line:** the R&D loop is closing with numbers attached — over 80% of Anthropic's code is now AI-merged, a 22-author position paper led by Hinton and Bengio is asking what happens next, and prompt-level memory just beat a fine-tuned model on a strict customer-service benchmark. Meanwhile China has put the one-person company into national policy, subsidies included. For a solo operator the playbook writes itself: own the specification, own the evaluation, run the keep/revert loop — and let the labs automate the middle of the chain while you keep the two ends.
