The one-person company news cycle on November 20 delivered three proof points at different scales. Everful, a 30-month-old company that replaced overseas sourcing teams with AI agents, closed $48.5 million and is tracking toward $100 million in annualized revenue. LATO, a Y Combinator startup selling agent-run market research to private equity, hit $1 million ARR within 100 days of its first paid project on a $4.7 million seed. And a solo game developer with almost no programming background sold more than 200,000 copies of an AI-assisted Steam game in under a week. Meanwhile, the US government's own auditors showed that four frontier chatbots can still be talked into producing viable attack plans within 12 hours — a reminder that the same agent capabilities powering these businesses are drawing regulatory attention.

## Key Insights

**Everful's $48.5 million Series A is the largest capital event of the day, and it is a bet on agents that operate a supply chain, not agents that write text.** The round was led by Vertex Ventures Southeast Asia & India and January Capital, with existing investors Square Peg and Lightspeed participating, per the company's November 20 announcement carried by PR Newswire and TechCrunch. Everful, founded in 2024 by Sisi Song (a former Alibaba Group executive and Bessemer Venture Partners investor) and Kevin Han (whose previous startup Orbeus was acquired by Amazon), connects small and mid-sized retailers directly to a vetted network of more than 30,000 factories. The operating numbers are concrete: the company ships more than 5 million items per month to retailers in over 60 countries, maintains a catalog of more than 20 million items, and is on track to reach $100 million in annualized revenue by the end of 2026 — less than two and a half years after launch.

The thesis in the founders' own words is instructive for anyone building agent products. Sisi Song framed the substitution directly: "Sourcing used to be a people problem. If you were big enough, you hired a team overseas to do it. AI lets us turn that team into software." Kevin Han drew the line between tools and agents that solo builders keep debating: "We don't build tools that leave the work to the customer. We build agents that deliver the result." Technically, the company runs what it calls a world model of supply and demand — mapping customer demand, factory capacity and logistics — that its agents use to spot trending products, vet factories and manage order communications, with each factory continuously scored from Everful's own inspection data. The fresh capital goes to US and European expansion plus deeper automation of customization workflows.

**LATO hit $1 million ARR in roughly 100 days on a $4.7 million seed, and its customer list shows agents moving into paid professional-services workflows.** The London-based startup, founded by Tymek Staniszewski (six years in growth equity and private equity at Verdane and Citi) and Tien Chu (AI and machine learning background including Samsung), came out of Y Combinator's Summer 2026 batch. The seed was co-led by Air Street Capital and Axiom Partners, with participation from Y Combinator, Montis VC, Inception Fund and SMOK Ventures — and an angel roster that includes Paul Graham plus founders of ElevenLabs, Vercel, Snowflake and Rogo. Since its first commercial research project in late July 2026, LATO's agents have completed more than 1,000 expert interviews across more than 35 countries, conducting them in native languages and combining findings with public research and client data. Customers — Verdane, Blume Equity and North Island, managing more than $500 billion in combined assets — use it for market research and commercial due diligence that previously took weeks. The significance: this is not a prosumer subscription business. It is agents selling into the most conservative buyer category there is, at seven figures of run rate within a quarter.

**First Person Labs exited stealth with roughly $10 million to build per-user models — personalization as infrastructure rather than a memory feature.** The company, founded seven months ago by two PhD students from Stanford's AI lab, closed about $10 million led by Andreessen Horowitz's infrastructure team, per Sina Finance's November report. Its pitch is a step beyond ChatGPT's memory and Claude Code's auto-recall: rather than storing preferences as retrievable text, it trains highly customized models and agents on a person's own data. If the approach holds up, it points at a build direction many solo developers can ride — personalization layers sold on top of general-purpose frontier models, the same way Postiz-style vertical SaaS sells on top of generic publishing tools.

## Market Trends

**Slotbound is the strongest solo-developer sales story of the quarter, and its tool stack is fully disclosed.** The roguelite auto-battler by solo developer Optima Arch launched on Steam on November 12 at $9.99 and, by the developer's November 19 announcement, had sold more than 200,000 copies in under a week. The trajectory per the developer's figures: about 50,000 copies and roughly $356,000 gross in the first 48 hours, over 100,000 copies by day three, 200,000-plus by day six or seven, with a launch discount at $8.99 running to about November 26. SteamDB recorded an independent peak of around 22,000 concurrent players — against roughly 2,900 for the summer demo, which reportedly built a wishlist above 100,000. Reviews sat near 79% positive from 800-plus reviews at the 100,000-copy mark.

The AI angle matters more than the sales number for this readership. The developer, who says he had almost no programming experience before starting, reports building the game with OpenAI's Codex for code, Gemini for design help, Nano Banana 2 and PixelLab for pixel art, and ElevenLabs for sound effects. Two caveats belong in every summary: the claim that the game is effectively fully AI-coded is the developer's own and cannot be independently verified, and the pre-launch demo audience gave the release a running start most first-time developers will not have. But the revenue shape — roughly $2 million gross in a week from a $9.99 title made by one person with agentic tooling — is the largest empirical datapoint yet for AI-assisted solo game development.

**The solo-builder tooling layer shipped monetization infrastructure this week.** Lovable's November 19 release added Adyen and Airwallex connectors covering checkout, payment links, refunds, subscriptions and invoices, removing one of the last build-it-yourself steps between a vibe-coded app and taking international payments. Factory Droid v1.15.0 (November 19) folds subagent sessions under their parent session for easier tracking. Antigravity CLI 1.5.13 (November 20) brings Gemini 4 Neon into its /model picker — with the team noting the context window was doubled in recent days and that Neon is faster but cache hit rates are still being improved. Grok Build hit v1.0.91, and OpenCode added GPT-6.2 prompt caching to cut repeat billing. The direction is unmistakable: the tooling race has moved from generating code to operating and monetizing products, with payments, session observability and inference cost control shipping as first-class features.

**The infrastructure layer under solo builders is tightening.** Capacity Media reported on November 19 that Nvidia has trimmed its neocloud commitments to $32 billion, with analysts flagging what tighter vendor support means for data-center landlords. Nothing here breaks the rental GPU market that agent operators depend on, but it is a second consecutive signal — after Musk's consolidation warning about 200-plus neoclouds — that the compute rental layer is entering a discipline phase. Operators with concentration risk on a single neocloud should take note.

## Build Directions

**Agent security is now a product category you can either adopt or build in.** Mitiga launched a dedicated AI Agent Security product on November 19 (PR Newswire) to stop rogue or malicious agents at machine speed, timed to a VentureBeat interview in which a former FBI cyber investigator said most security teams cannot spot a rogue AI agent in their environment. For solo operators the practical version is simpler: log every agent action, scope every credential, and alert on out-of-pattern behavior. For builders, the gap between "agents shipped by enterprises" and "agents monitored by enterprises" is an obvious services wedge.

**Offline and auditable agents are an underserved regulatory niche.** PraisonAI's v4.7.26 release adds fully offline agent execution plus an early-access handover mode for highly regulated teams, letting AI tasks pass to self-hosted models with a verifiable audit trail. The buyer is the same one LATO is winning: organizations that need the work done but cannot explain to a regulator which model touched which record.

**The scientist-in-the-loop repository pattern is replicable in any standardized domain.** Kenneth D. Harris released the International Brain Lab AI Agent, an MIT-licensed open-source project that wraps domain data, analysis conventions and publishing pipelines around ordinary coding agents such as OpenAI Codex and Claude Code, operating in collaborative scientist-in-the-loop mode. Any field with standardized datasets and codified analysis workflows — accounting, compliance, logistics, education — can copy the architecture. It is a template for vertical agent products that does not require training anything.

**Per-user model personalization is a wedge worth watching.** First Person Labs' funding validates demand for personalization deeper than context memory. Solo builders should watch what its API surface looks like when it ships; the same techniques on a smaller scale — per-customer fine-tunes for a niche workflow — remain buildable today with open-weight models and modest GPU rentals.

## Risk Factors

**The GAO's red-team report is the week's most consequential safety disclosure.** The Government Accountability Office reported this week, per Forbes, that investigators were able to obtain largely "viable" real-world attack plans from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok in under 12 hours of trying — two models produced blueprints for taking down parts of the power grid, others gave mass-casualty bombing instructions. Safeguards triggered in each case, but were bypassed with common techniques, and investigators verified plans against real-world locations and experts. Anthropic responded that its protections have improved since the tests were performed this spring and questioned the tests' legitimacy; the GAO itself conceded the exercise was not a scientific comparison and some plans contained flaws. The Times of London, citing the broader reporting effort, counted at least 70 deaths from attacks planned with AI assistance. Expect this report to be cited in every pending bill — including the one Anthropic is lobbying for.

**Anthropic is actively pushing a specific safety bill, and the pacing debate is now formal policy advocacy.** Per Politico-based reporting, Anthropic has since June 2026 pitched legislation based on its "Advanced AI Framework" to federal and state lawmakers, advocating independent pre-release testing of frontier models. Dario Amodei's essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" warns that an unmanaged "swarm" of AI agents could cause hundreds of billions of dollars in damage within a six-to-twelve-month window, and argues that buying even one or two extra years for alignment work would materially reduce catastrophic risk. Sam Altman and Elon Musk have both voiced agreement that the trajectory needs slowing — a rare three-way consensus that makes some form of frontier-model legislation more likely in 2027. Separately, Geoffrey Hinton warned lawmakers to act now after a closed-door Capitol Hill briefing on November 19. Solo builders deploying agents should plan for identity, logging and kill-switch requirements migrating from proposals into platform terms of service well before any statute arrives.

**Google quietly quadrupled Gemini chat retention, and the privacy baseline is moving.** TechCrunch reported that Google is extending retention of temporary Gemini conversations from 72 hours to roughly 60 days globally over the coming weeks, citing safety response and incident diagnosis, while decoupling chat history storage from training consent into separate controls. Google's 60-day default now sits at double OpenAI's 30-day temporary-chat policy and Anthropic's 30-day incognito default. Teams embedding Gemini in regulated workflows — healthcare, finance, legal — may need documented data-handling agreements that account for the two-month window, and the precedent matters: if extended retention becomes the "industry standard," regulators may codify it.

**Survivorship bias remains the meta-risk of this entire genre.** Slotbound's developer disclosed unverifiable claims, had a 100,000-wishlist head start, and still only cleared 79% positive reviews; LATO's 100-day ARR is a run-rate snapshot, not retention data; Everful's $100M annualized target is company-projected. The base rate for solo AI products has not moved — these are the outliers the algorithm surfaced.

## Action Recommendations

**Study Everful's substitution math, not just its funding.** "Hire a team overseas, then turn it into software" is a repeatable test: any workflow currently staffed by a distributed human team — sourcing, moderation, bookkeeping, localization QA — is a candidate for the same agent-first rebuild. Price the human team's annual cost first; that number is your market ceiling.

**Wire payments on day one using the new connectors.** Lovable's Adyen and Airwallex integrations mean a solo-built app can support cards, refunds, subscriptions and invoices without a payments engineering sprint. If your product has been waiting on monetization plumbing, the excuse expired on November 19.

**Copy the IBL repository pattern into one vertical you know.** Take a domain with standardized data and written conventions, wrap it in an AGENTS.md-style repo, and sell the analysis output rather than the tool. It costs a weekend and no model training.

**Add minimal agent auditing now, ahead of regulation.** Log agent identity, actions and model per step; scope credentials per task; alert on out-of-pattern behavior. Mitiga's launch and the GAO report point the same direction — enterprises will be asked to prove which agent did what, and the audit trail is cheap to build before it is required.

**Re-check any Gemini integration against the 60-day retention change.** If you process client data through temporary chats or API transcripts, update your data-handling documentation and confirm the training-consent toggle state on accounts you control before the global rollout completes.
