Three developers, three products, and one shared decision: none of them bet on subscriptions. Lorenzo Cabra turned a library of interactive React components into roughly $39,000 within about three weeks of opening paid access — most of it from €129 one-time purchases. A solo engineer in India has quietly collected about $106,000 over four years selling chip-design interview challenges, and his monthly membership sold exactly zero. And a Toronto founder building a to-do app publishes his $8 MRR next to a $59.99 lifetime plan, because honest numbers are part of the product. If you can build something developers want to copy-paste, the pay-once model may be the fastest cash path a one-person company has right now.

## This Week's Playbook

### Bencho — interactive React components, paid in three weeks

**Who:** Lorenzo Cabra, a solo front-end developer, with 7,170 followers on X and an open GitHub repo.

**What:** Bencho (bencho.dev) is a library of 160+ interactive React components and micro-interactions — hover states, drag gestures, typing effects, loading patterns — that you can explore live in the browser, tweak, and copy straight into your project. There is also a library of 140+ UI sounds and an MCP server, so AI coding assistants can search the library and insert blocks into your codebase.

**The numbers (Dodo Payments verified, Nov 22, 2026):** $39,251 all-time revenue, $23,008 in the last 30 days (up 47% over the previous period), paid access opened roughly three weeks ago. The subscription part is almost an afterthought: 54 active subscriptions and $432 MRR. The rest — the overwhelming majority — is one-time money. Reported profit margin: 80%.

**Pricing:** Free tier with 47 blocks and 24 sounds; Pro at €99/year; "Forever" at €129 once, no renewal, future content included. The lifetime tier is doing the heavy lifting.

**Distribution:** Content marketing and SEO on a site pulling about 300K page views a month, plus X and LinkedIn posts, plus the GitHub repo as a trust anchor. Domain rating 31 — built, not bought.

**Stack:** Next.js, TypeScript, Supabase, Cloudflare, Resend, Dodo Payments. Cloud-tier hosting costs are negligible; the 80% margin tells you the total bill.

**Timeline:** Audience and free library first, monetization last — the component library accumulated page views and followers for months before the paywall went up, and then converted in weeks.

### QuickSilicon — chip design interview challenges, $106K over four years

**Who:** A solo engineer in India who runs QuickSilicon, a platform where engineers practice real chip-design problems — RTL design and verification challenges — and get back actual synthesis and timing reports, not a pass/fail quiz.

**The numbers (Razorpay verified):** roughly $106,000 cumulative over about four years, from around 8,000 engineers who have used the platform. Notably, the founder discloses that after launching a monthly membership, the payment records show zero subscription revenue — engineers buy individual challenge packs and pay-per-use instead, and the recurring plan just sits there.

**The lesson:** this is the mirror image of the Bencho story, from a completely different niche, arriving at the same conclusion. A highly technical audience with a hard problem (cracking RTL interview loops at places like Tenstorrent, whose engineers now publish PPA challenges on the platform) will pay for concrete, verifiable practice — but they will not subscribe to it.

### Task Pocket — what honest numbers look like

**Who:** Bohdan Stefaniuk, solo founder in Toronto, founded April 2026.

**What:** A deliberately small to-do app — inbox, today, upcoming, projects grouped into areas, natural-language parsing ("water the plants every other Saturday at 10am"), browser plus offline iPhone app. No AI inside. Instead: a public API and an MCP server so you can attach your own AI.

**The numbers (Stripe verified, Nov 21, 2026):** $193 all-time, $8 MRR, 2 active subscriptions. Pricing: free plan, $3.99/month, $34.99/year, or $59.99 once.

**Why it is in here:** because most pay-once experiments do not look like Bencho's first three weeks, and the platforms now verify that publicly. Task Pocket's listing — free-tier generous, lifetime option available, ASO and Product Hunt and Hacker News as channels, DR 12 — is what the median first-year dev tool actually earns. The model is the same as Bencho's; the execution surface is smaller. Put both on your wall: one is the ceiling, the other is the floor, and both are true at the same time.

## How to Copy This

The strongest replication target here is Bencho, because every input is observable: the site, the pricing, the repo, the free-tier limits.

1. **Pick a category where developers judge quality in 10 seconds.** Bencho chose UI micro-interactions: you see the component animating, you know instantly if it is good. QuickSilicon chose interview problems with real synthesis reports. Choose a niche where your artifact *demonstrates itself*.
2. **Build the free layer as the marketing.** 47 free blocks out of 160+. Free tier limits are the distribution budget — generous enough to be shared, tight enough that heavy users pay. Cost: $0 beyond your time.
3. **Ship "copy-paste" as the core feature, not AI.** Every block is live, tweakable, and paste-ready. The MCP server rides the existing AI-assistant wave without paying for a model — the user brings their own subscription.
4. **Price the lifetime plan higher than feels comfortable.** €129 once versus €99/year means the lifetime plan pays for itself in 16 months — and most buyers in the first wave are exactly the people who will never churn into it. The €129 one-timer is where the three weeks of revenue came from.
5. **Route billing through an indie-first MoR.** Bencho uses Dodo Payments, Task Pocket uses Stripe, QuickSilicon uses Razorpay. Pick the one that covers your customers' countries and handles tax, so a €129 purchase from anywhere is one checkout away.
6. **Publish the numbers.** Verified revenue dashboards are themselves a marketing channel now — the TrustMRR/AI Receipts ecosystem links back, and "payment-verified" beats "7-figure founder" for trust.

**Costs to expect:** domain + hosting under $20/month on Cloudflare free tiers; Supabase free tier until you have real traffic; payment platform fees 3-5% + payout costs. Total pre-revenue spend: well under $100 plus your evenings.

**Pitfalls:** the component library took months of free publishing before any revenue — if you paywall a library nobody has bookmarked yet, nothing converts. And the license matters: Bencho allows use in commercial projects but forbids reselling the blocks as-is; write yours before launch, not after the first scraper.

**Your first step this week:** open a file and list 20 things developers in one narrow niche copy-paste by hand — SQL snippets, regex patterns, chart configs, CSS layouts, test fixtures. Circle the three you know cold. That list is your component library; next week you build the first ten free ones in public.

## Micro-Opportunities

- **Copy-paste libraries for non-JS stacks.** Bencho owns React interactions. Nobody owns the equivalent for Swift/SwiftUI micro-interactions, Flutter animations, or shadcn-compatible chart configs. Same pricing: free 30, €99/year, €129 lifetime. Starting cost: a weekend and a $12 domain.
- **Interview-prep platforms for trades with no simulator.** QuickSilicon did chip design because no one else gave real synthesis feedback. SQL performance profiling, Kubernetes troubleshooting with real clusters, embedded C debugging in-browser — every one has candidates who would pay $20 per graded attempt. Start with one challenge type.
- **UI sound packs with permissive licensing.** 140+ interface sounds was enough to be a paid feature, not a product. Sourcing and licensing sound libraries for app UIs is a sub-$100 side project with a near-zero marginal cost.
- **MCP servers for existing content libraries.** Any searchable asset collection — components, sounds, snippets, templates — becomes AI-assistant-distributable with a thin MCP wrapper. If you already own the content, the wrapper is a weekend.
- **Lifetime-deal audits for solo founders.** The €129-forever math (16-month crossover) generalizes to any subscription product. A small calculator-plus-template site for indie makers pricing their first product: free tool, paid spreadsheet, pay-once pricing modeled on itself.

## Signals Worth Knowing

- **Indie-first payment rails are now verification infrastructure, not just checkout.** Dodo Payments verified Bencho's $39K, Razorpay verified QuickSilicon's $106K, RevenueCat verified the app cases, and TrustMRR syndicates all of them into leaderboards that journalists and aggregators quote. What it means for you: pick a billing platform with a public verification story early — the dashboard becomes a distribution channel that no payment fee schedule can price.
- **The MCP wrapper is becoming the new freemium hook.** Bencho gates its MCP server behind Pro; Task Pocket ships one for a $8-MRR app; CallCloud added one so AI agents can dial. The pattern costs a weekend and rides other people's AI subscriptions. What it means for you: if your product has a searchable catalog, an MCP endpoint is the cheapest new distribution surface of 2026 — and "bring your own AI" keeps your costs at zero.
