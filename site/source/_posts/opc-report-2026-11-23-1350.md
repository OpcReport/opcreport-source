The most reliable solo-founder revenue on the verified boards this week is not an AI product — it is a dialer. CallCloud, run by one person since October 2022, just verified about $16,500 in 30 days from 183 subscriptions by doing two unglamorous things: undercutting every published price in its category, and showing up in the places where AI assistants answer "what's the cheapest parallel dialer." Alongside it, a three-month-old video rewriting tool is compounding at 274% a month, and two Estonian twins sell bar trivia nights to venues at €199 a month. Different trades, same pattern: pick a workflow a specific business already does daily, make one step measurably better, price per seat or per venue.

## This Week's Playbook

### CallCloud — a solo-built dialer that sales teams pay for every month

**Who:** Richard Tattory, one person, United States. Founded October 2022 — four years in, no team page, no funding round.

**What:** CallCloud (callcloud.app) is a browser-based parallel dialer for outbound sales teams. Reps dial up to five prospects at once and connect the instant someone picks up — "zero lag" is the pitch. Unlimited US and Canada calling, local numbers included, AI call notes and an AI score on every call, native sync with thirteen CRMs and sequencers (HubSpot, Salesforce, GoHighLevel, Pipedrive, Apollo, Outreach, Close, Attio and more), plus iPhone and iPad apps. Over 930,000 calls dialed in the last 30 days.

**The numbers (Stripe verified):** $280,304 all-time revenue; 183 active subscriptions; The AI Receipts' 30-day read on November 20 came to $16.5K, which matches the seat math — 183 seats at roughly $60-$125 each. TrustMRR's MRR field reads $33,022 as of November 22; take the conservative $16.5K/month figure as the floor.

**Pricing — the actual weapon:** Power Dialer $60/seat/month, Parallel Dialer $125/seat/month (up to five lines), CallCloud+ add-on +$40/seat. Month-to-month, no annual contract, free three-day trial with no card. Compare the category: Trellus publishes $99.99-$149.99 per seat, WAVV $149, PhoneBurner $165, Salesfinity $299. CallCloud prints a price 40-60% below all of them and lets teams walk away monthly.

**Distribution:** LinkedIn posts and SEO, plus — and this is the 2026 part — AI search. The founder's own reporting and The AI Receipts' case study both point to ChatGPT-style answers as a live lead channel: when a sales manager asks an assistant to recommend a budget parallel dialer, CallCloud's transparent published pricing makes it the answer the model can actually quote.

**Stack:** published as Stripe plus calling infrastructure. The plumbing — carrier compliance, spam ratings, CRM write-back — took years, and that unsexy depth is the moat.

**Timeline:** four years from launch to $280K cumulative, with the AI-notes layer and MCP-era integrations added on top of a working dialer — the AI rides the tool, not the reverse.

### Aisma — three months in, +274% month over month

**Who:** A solo founder (TrustMRR listing: Aisma Lab), product at aisma.

**What:** a one-sentence rewrite editor for footage you have already shot — upload a finished video, change what it says, and the tool re-renders speech to match. Aimed at creators and small businesses who cannot afford a reshoot.

**The numbers (Stripe verified):** $4,993 cumulative in about three months; last 30 days $3,928, up 274% over the previous period; MRR $1,948; 35 subscriptions. Small in absolute terms, but the curve is the point: this is what the month-three slope looks like for a tool that solves an expensive, emotionally urgent problem (reshoots cost money nobody budgeted).

**The lesson:** you do not need $16.5K a month for the pattern to work. One painful step in someone's workflow, one AI capability wrapped around it, priced as a work tool — and the verified leaderboard becomes your progress bar.

### Quizado — selling the event, not the software

**Who:** Twin brothers in Estonia (team of two), running Quizado.

**What:** they took the American bar trivia night — normally an expensive third-party booking — and turned it into a subscription: a hosted quiz platform that venues run themselves.

**The numbers:** roughly $3,989 in the last 30 days from 27 paying venues, at €199/month list price, with 5,000 hosts and users across 150+ countries around the product.

**The lesson:** B2B pricing does not have to follow SaaS seat conventions. A venue that books one trivia night pays a host hundreds of dollars; €199/month to run unlimited nights in-house is an easy yes. Same logic applies to every "service you can productize" — wedding DJ packs, gym class plans, market stall inventory sheets.

## How to Copy This

CallCloud is the hardest of the three to replicate outright (calling infrastructure takes years), so copy the *pricing and distribution* decisions instead, and pair them with a product you can build in a month.

1. **Choose a workflow with a daily-use tool and a published-price cartel.** Dialers, schedulers, transcription, invoicing: incumbents hide pricing or charge $150+. Find a category where "request a demo" is the norm — that is your opening. Cost of research: one afternoon.
2. **Print your price.** Every comparison site, and increasingly every AI assistant, can only recommend products with public prices. Month-to-month beats annual contracts for small teams; CallCloud's no-commitment stance is a conversion feature, not lost revenue.
3. **Price 40-60% below the cheapest named incumbent.** You have no sales team, no office, no support staff to fund. $60 against $99.99 is the entire pitch in one number.
4. **Add AI where it saves the user's time, not as the product.** AI call notes and call scores are worth $40/seat on top of a dialer because they replace post-call admin. The tool still works if the AI is mediocre — that is the difference between a feature and a dependency.
5. **Be quotable.** Transparent pricing pages, a short trial with no card, and clear "for insurance agencies / SDR teams / recruiters" landing pages give both human referrers and AI models exactly what they need to name you. CallCloud's leads come from LinkedIn *and* from assistants doing the comparison for buyers.
6. **Start at Aisma's scale and publish the slope.** $4K months with +274% growth, verified, is enough to attract attention, affiliates, and eventually acquisition interest. Verification is the flywheel: Stripe or an MoR like Dodo feeds TrustMRR, which feeds the aggregators, which feed the AI answers.

**Costs to expect:** a web product on Supabase/Vercel free tiers plus Stripe (2.9% + 30¢) starts near zero. Avoid categories with per-use infrastructure (telephony, SMS, video streaming) for your first product — that is where the years of plumbing live.

**Pitfalls:** per-seat pricing with no annual plan attracts churn-prone customers — watch net revenue, not gross signups. And do not let the AI layer become the moat: CallCloud's defense is four years of CRM integrations and carrier plumbing, not prompts.

**Your first step this week:** pick one business type you can name five real examples of (dental clinics, real-estate photographers, cafés with trivia nights). Call or message two of them and ask what repetitive tool they pay for that annoys them and what it costs. One real quote from a real buyer is worth more than a month of idea lists.

## Micro-Opportunities

- **Undercut-one-product audits.** Pick any crowded B2B category with quote-only pricing (employee scheduling, field service dispatch, proposal software). A single-product clone at a published 50% lower seat price, month-to-month, is a viable solo wedge — the category demand already exists.
- **Reshoot insurance for content teams.** Aisma's wedge: video is expensive to redo. The adjacent openings — subtitle re-timing, product-shot relighting, voice replacement for compliance updates — all monetize "already-shot footage" and cost nothing to demo on portfolios.
- **Venue-priced event kits.** Quizado's €199/month works anywhere a venue currently books a human: quiz nights, painting classes, kids' party hosts. One template product, one venue type, per-venue pricing instead of per-seat.
- **AI-notes layers for vertical CRMs.** CallCloud charges +$40/seat for call notes. Every vertical tool with an interaction log (property management, tutoring centers, mobile detailing) lacks one. Sell the notes layer to the existing tool's users instead of competing with the tool.
- **"Quotable pricing" consulting for solo founders.** Most indie B2B products still hide prices. A checklist-plus-service that rewrites a pricing page to be AI-assistant-friendly (published rates, no-card trial, comparison-page copy) charges $300-500 a project and teaches you the channel while you sell it.

## Signals Worth Knowing

- **AI search is now a measurable B2B lead channel with a documented case.** CallCloud's founder attributes live leads to ChatGPT-style answers naming his product, because transparent published pricing makes it quotable. What it means for you: your pricing page is now an SEO asset in a second search ecosystem — publish numbers, keep trial friction at zero, and structure landing pages by buyer type so assistants can cite you.
- **The seat-price floor in sales tooling keeps dropping while incumbents quote-only.** CallCloud at $60/seat month-to-month against PhoneBurner's $165 and Salesfinity's $299 shows solo operators can sustainably price below every published competitor and still clear five figures a month. What it means for you: when choosing a niche, prefer ones where the incumbent's price is not a price — the unquoted category is the underpriced category.
