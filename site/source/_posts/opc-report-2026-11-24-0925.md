Three payment-verified numbers landed this week, and each one kills an excuse. A Chinese solo founder sells instruction packs for AI coding agents and just crossed $6,085 MRR — roughly triple what he reported six weeks ago, from a product that cost nothing to produce. A UK founder with no disclosed coding background built a home-renovation visualization app that pulls in $8,179 MRR from exactly one acquisition channel: TikTok carousels of before-and-after rooms, zero ad spend. And a British developer who lost his biggest client in February now makes $3,014 MRR from AI tools that run entirely on the user's device, meaning his model bill is $0 forever. All three fit inside the under-$300-to-start, one-person, sustainable bracket. Here is how each one actually works.

## This Week's Blueprint

**Skillry — Yihui (@yihui_indie), one person, roughly $0 invested, $6,085 verified MRR in month four** (TrustMRR, Stripe-verified, November 19 sync; founder's posts on X). Skillry sells Agent Skills: packaged instruction sets that plug into Claude Code, Codex and Cursor so the agent follows a fixed, professional workflow — producing websites, slides, images or video in a consistent style instead of improvising. The product is literally knowledge work: markdown files describing how an expert would sequence the task. The trust mechanic is the clever part — every skill's real output is previewable before you install, so the buyer judges the product by what it makes, not by a landing page. The revenue trajectory is the steepest we have tracked in this category: $1,673 MRR on October 8, $4,184 by October 30, $6,085 with 475 subscriptions by November 19, $12,188 collected in the last 30 days and $21,337 all-time. (We first covered Skillry on October 19 at $2,170 MRR; everything above the October numbers is the increment.) Pricing is a three-rung ladder: a free tier that does the demand testing, Premium at $9.99 a month or $79 a year, and a $169 lifetime plan for the people who hate subscriptions. Real revenue began around late September when the paid tier went live; the founder posted on October 22 that cumulative revenue had passed $10,000 about three weeks in. Total startup cost: his existing Claude Code subscription and time.

**House Goals — founder Jordan, UK, $8,179 MRR five months after launch, acquired exclusively through TikTok** (TrustMRR, RevenueCat-verified, November 20 update; Starter Story interview, October 4; TrustMRR listing page). House Goals shows homeowners what a renovation would look like before they spend on one: upload a room photo, see the transformed version, then decide whether to buy furniture, paint, or hire a designer. The scope is deliberately narrow — show the after, sell the confidence. The numbers: launched June 2026 with $277 in the first month, roughly $20,685 collected in August alone, and a verified current state of $8,179 MRR from 623 subscriptions, $9,124 in the last 30 days and $60,532 all-time. Distribution is the entire story here: the only channel is TikTok, and the only content is before-and-after carousels — the product's own output doubles as the ad creative, and no paid ads run at all. Monetization is a hard paywall with no free tier: $12.99 per week or $39.99 per year, no monthly option — the weekly price makes trying it cheap while the annual price anchors it as a bargain. Two honest caveats: the founder's background and build stack are not publicly documented, so do not assume vibe coding; and ad spend is undisclosed (though he states the channel is organic TikTok). The kicker: he listed the app for sale on TrustMRR on November 17 at $600,000 — about six times annualized revenue — because he has too many projects. Build-then-sell is a live exit at this size.

**Yaps — Rich (@RichDoesTech), one person, bootstrapped, $3,014 MRR with a $0 model bill** (TrustMRR, Stripe-verified, November 19; profitable.app; yaps.ai). Rich had built products before (Nack AI among them), but Yaps started in March 2026 for the least glamorous reason possible: his biggest client ran out of money in February. Yaps is a suite of AI utilities — dictation, speech generation, notes, captioning, transcription — where the models run on the user's device. Audio and notes never leave the machine, which is both the privacy pitch and the cost structure: no per-user inference bill, ever. It ships on Mac, Windows, Linux, Android and iOS. TrustMRR reads $3,014 MRR from 226 active subscriptions and $17,525 all-time, with profitable.app confirming the $15/month or $144/year Pro tier and fully bootstrapped status. The wedge is a genuinely free Android voice-typing keyboard ($0 forever, runs locally) that feeds the paid desktop suite — a competitor's independent teardown (Lexo Keyboard, August 2026) confirmed the local-first claims check out, which in this market is rare enough to be a moat. Honest weak spot: a 2.7/5 rating across 47 Google Play reviews, and $3K MRR is the smallest number on this page — but it is also the most literal template: one developer, local open-weight models, a privacy promise, five platforms.

## 0 to 1 Steps

The five-question self-diagnosis first — all three founders pass cleanly:

1. What am I genuinely good at? (Yihui: squeezing professional workflows out of coding agents. Jordan: seeing what a room could become. Rich: shipping cross-platform utilities.)
2. Which niche group has a painful, repeated task? (Agent users rebuilding the same workflow daily; homeowners paralyzed before renovating; anyone who types more than they write.)
3. Can AI make the work 10x cheaper or faster? (Skillry: the customer's agent does the labor. Yaps: the customer's device does the compute. House Goals: one photo replaces a design consultation.)
4. What is broken about existing solutions? (Generic agents improvise inconsistently; renovation decisions are made blind; cloud AI tools leak your audio and charge monthly forever.)
5. Can I ship an MVP in three months? (Skillry's paid tier was live in about six weeks; House Goals launched in its first month.)

Then the 30-day sprint, with the cases' real numbers as calibration:

- **Week 1 — interviews and output tests.** Talk to at least 10 people in your niche. Then run the decisive experiment: produce five before/after samples by hand (renovations, documents, code reviews — whatever your niche's "after" is). If strangers react to the after-picture without explanation, House Goals' mechanism works for you. Cost: $0.
- **Week 2 — build the thinnest deliverable.** Three shapes, cheapest first: a skill/instruction pack (days of writing, $0), a photo-in/visualization-out app (no-code builder + RevenueCat, ~$100), or a local-model utility (whisper-class models, $0 inference). Ship one platform, one job. Cost: under $100.
- **Week 3 — let the output market itself.** Post the before/after pairs daily on TikTok or the platform where your niche scrolls. Make the real output previewable before purchase — Skillry's preview-before-install is why 475 people subscribe. Add one free-forever wedge feature, Yaps-style. Cost: $0.
- **Week 4 — paywall from day one.** All three charged early: hard paywall with a weekly price under an annual anchor (House Goals), or free tier → $9.99/mo → lifetime (Skillry). Target one paying customer. Keep every payment on RevenueCat or Stripe so your revenue is third-party verifiable — that verification is what later turns into a $600K listing. Cost: $0.

**Your first step this week:** open your AI coding agent, pick the one workflow you repeat most — the way you always set up a repo, always format a document, always review a contract — and write it out as a numbered step-by-step instruction pack. Publish it free. If people install it, you have validated demand for the paid version of exactly this play, at a total cost of one afternoon.

## Micro-Opportunities

1. **Skill packs for one profession's agent workflows.** Target: lawyers, accountants, QA engineers and analysts who drive Claude Code, Codex or Cursor daily but rebuild the same prompt sequence every session. Entry: package their workflow as a previewable skill, free tier plus $9.99/mo. Starting cost: ~$0 — this is Skillry's exact ladder, still only months old with room in every vertical.
2. **Before/after visualization for a different vertical.** Target: garden redesign, hairstyles, fitness transformations, furniture placement — anywhere a photo "after" is self-explanatory and the decision that follows is expensive. Entry: no-code builder, hard weekly paywall, TikTok carousels of your own output. Starting cost: ~$100/month in tools; House Goals proves no ad budget is required.
3. **An on-device privacy tool for one regulated audience.** Target: doctors, lawyers, therapists, journalists — people whose recordings cannot touch a cloud server. Entry: wrap local speech models into one job (meeting notes, dictation, transcription) on desktop first, Android free wedge second. Starting cost: ~$0 model bill; Yaps runs the whole playbook at $3K MRR.
4. **AI-crawler audit and fix for small business sites.** Target: SMB owners whose sites silently block ChatGPT's crawler — of 640 sites tested in a recent audit, 100 blocked AI crawlers and only 4 had declared it in robots.txt (details in Signals). Entry: a $50-100 detection script plus a fixed-price "detect + repair" package sold to local businesses. Starting cost: ~$50.
5. **Free-forever wedge into a paid cross-platform suite.** Target: any utility where one feature can be genuinely free (Yaps' Android voice keyboard) while the pro features live in a paid desktop app. Entry: ship the free wedge on the platform with the most users, the paid suite where the power users are. Starting cost: ~$0 — your distribution is the review section of the free app.

## Signals Worth Knowing

**Open-weight models now carry almost a third of enterprise token traffic (SaaStr, reading Databricks Unity Gateway data, November 20):** open-weight share of enterprise tokens jumped from 7.3% to 30.9% in four months, single-provider dependence fell from 64% to 28%, and GLM 5.2 alone accounts for about 40% of open-weight traffic. **What it means for you:** the cost floor under any AI product keeps dropping — route your high-volume, low-difficulty inference (classification, summarization, formatting) to open-weight models and your gross margin starts looking like Yaps', even if you stay in the cloud. COGS is now a product decision, not a fixed tax.

**Most sites are accidentally invisible to AI search (HackerNoon audit, November 20):** the author tested 640 websites and found 100 blocking AI crawlers — but only 4 had declared it in robots.txt; the other 96 were silently blocked by Cloudflare or host-level firewalls. ChatGPT cites third-party reviews over vendor sites precisely because it can parse them, so being accidentally blocked means being absent from the answers your buyers read. **What it means for you:** spend one hour this week checking whether OAI-SearchBot and the major agent crawlers can actually reach your site (remember robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to propagate) — and if you can write the detection script, item 4 above is a service business hiding in this statistic.
