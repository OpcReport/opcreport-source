Most revenue curves you see on the verified boards go up and to the right. This week's most instructive one went sideways for two years first. Visualizee, an AI rendering tool for architects built and run by one person in Poland, sat at $100-150 a month from mid-2023 to late 2025 — twenty-nine months of near silence. Then the founder deleted his node-based interface, replaced it with a chat box that asks follow-up questions, moved from one-time pricing to subscriptions, and six months later Stripe was reading $8,474 a month from 297 active subscribers. Alongside it, two verified listings show the other side of the same lesson: a React Native template shop that made $88,000 at a 90% margin before AI coding tools ate its market, and a SwiftUI self-tracking app whose founder ran every dollar of growth through paid ads and is now selling it for half a year's revenue. Three one-person businesses, three payment-verified numbers, and one shared takeaway: what you charge for and how users talk to it matters more than the model underneath.

## This Week's Blueprint

**Visualizee (visualizee.ai) — Piotr Obidowski, Poland, one person, architect AI rendering, from ~$150/month flat for two years to $8,600 MRR after one pivot** (1k.com.cn case study, November 18, 2026; corroborated by The AI Receipts design-tools leaderboard, November 23: $7,300 MRR, 270 subs, ~$27 per subscriber, Stripe-verified on TrustMRR). Visualizee turns floor plans, sketches and SketchUp screenshots into photoreal renders for architects and interior designers. The first version, launched around August 2023, copied the ComfyUI pattern: a node editor where users wired up their own generation pipeline. It hit #5 on Product Hunt on launch day — and then earned $100 to $150 a month, every month, for roughly two years. In late 2025, Obidowski made two changes. First, he deleted the nodes entirely and replaced them with Vizzy, a chat assistant that proactively asks about style, lighting and materials instead of exposing knobs. Second, he moved from one-time payments to a three-tier subscription — $15, $35 and $80 a month — with a 7-day trial. Six months on: $8,600 MRR, with the late-October Stripe read at $8,474 from 297 active subscriptions and $93,419 collected all-time. The kicker: he never quit his job. This is still a nights-and-weekends business, which means its economics are pure side income — the tool prints roughly a median European salary on top of employment. Honest caveats: The AI Receipts shows a -6.7% 30-day trend on the current read, and architects judge render quality instantly, so the craft bar is real. The lesson is not "build an AI render tool"; it is that a flat revenue curve for two years was a UX problem and a pricing problem, not a demand problem — architects always needed renders.

**Native Templates (native-templates.com) — Tomas Zeman, one person since April 2025, $88,221 all-time at ~90% margin, now for sale because AI ate the market** (TrustMRR listing, Polar-verified, updated November 25, 2026). Zeman sells 26 premium React Native templates — complete booking, e-commerce, fitness, AI-chat and ride-sharing app UIs in Expo and TypeScript, two libraries (NativeWind and Uniwind), a full-stack boilerplate, plus MCP servers for Claude and Cursor. One-time pricing: $59-$99 per template, $119 per library bundle, $149 for everything, lifetime updates. 500+ customers, recommended by NativeWind itself and featured on Expo, distributed via SEO on a domain rating of 32. The numbers: $88,221 all-time revenue at roughly 90% margin — no servers to run, no models to pay for, just files — but the last-30-day read is only $1,278, down 23%, and the founder's own listing message says sales slowed in 2026 "as AI coding tools changed how devs build UI." He is selling the whole thing — source, two domains, the 500-customer email list, the MCP servers, the X account — to focus on a new product. This is the counter-case to Visualizee: a product whose job was to save developers from writing UI code got structurally repriced to zero by the very same AI wave, and the founder had the discipline to read the curve and exit rather than pump ads into a dying category. Note the asymmetry: Visualizee's customers (architects) buy outcomes — a photorealistic render they cannot produce themselves. Native Templates' customers (developers) buy labor-saving code, and AI now does that labor free.

**Private Venture — Vincy, United Kingdom, one person, iOS self-tracking app, $1,455 MRR built entirely on paid ads, listed for sale at $22,500** (TrustMRR listing, RevenueCat-verified, November 25, 2026; founder message). Private Venture tracks your day automatically on iPhone — activity, workouts, energy, mood — with a built-in AI companion, no wearable required, all data on-device. Native SwiftUI, subscriptions with free trial via RevenueCat, pricing at $4.99/week or $29.99/year. Founded July 2026, bootstrapped, one person: $13,604 all-time revenue, 364 active subscriptions, $4,120 in the last 30 days (+24%), 2,240 total users across top markets US, Japan and Australia — fully localized in Japanese, 60+ five-star reviews. What makes this listing worth reading is the founder's own diagnosis: "Every user came from Meta and Apple Search Ads I run myself. No UGC, influencers or organic content yet, so the biggest growth channels are completely untouched." He is selling at $22,500 — about 0.5x annualized revenue — arguing the buyer inherits an app with organic channels nobody has tried. This is the floor case in this week's set: it proves a solo founder can acquire hundreds of paying subscribers with $5 weekly pricing and self-run ads, and it also shows what a business with rented distribution looks like on the block — cheap, because the audience leaves when the ad spend stops.

The shared pattern: all three are one person, all three verify their revenue through a public payment-API key (Stripe, Polar, RevenueCat), and all three live or die by the same variable — whether the customer's alternative is a human doing the work (renders: yes, templates: no) and whether the distribution is owned or rented.

| Payments | Stripe or Polar | Stripe for subscriptions (Visualizee); Polar for one-time files (Native Templates) — both API-readable, which doubles as marketing | 2.9-4% + fixed fee per transaction |
| Templates/dev tools | Expo + TypeScript, sold once | Native Templates' 90% margin comes from selling files: no hosting, no support debt beyond lifetime updates | $0 marginal cost per sale |
| Marketing: owned | SEO + blog | Visualizee and Native Templates both list SEO first; a DR of 21-32 is enough when the niche is narrow | $0 + your time |

The stack column's recurring lesson: two of the three founders sell outcomes priced monthly ($15-80), one sells files priced once ($59-149) — and the file-seller is the one exiting. Subscription to an outcome beat one-time sale of an artifact in 2026, because the artifact's price keeps falling.

## 0 to 1 Steps

The five-question self-diagnosis, calibrated against this week's cases:

1. What am I genuinely good at? (Obidowski: architecture-domain knowledge plus enough code to ship. Zeman: production React Native patterns. Vincy: SwiftUI and ad account management.)
2. Which niche buys an outcome it cannot produce itself? (Architects cannot photoreal-render their own sketches; homeowners cannot picture renovations; developers can now code UI themselves — which is why the template shop is for sale.)
3. Can AI make it 10x faster or cheaper? (A render that took a visualization studio days costs cents of API. The templates that took weeks to write now get written by the customer's AI — the 10x cut both ways.)
4. What is broken about existing solutions? (Node editors expose pipeline complexity; visualization studios cost hundreds per render and take days; manual self-tracking is tedious.)
5. Can I ship an MVP in three months? (Visualizee's pivot itself — delete UI, add a chat loop, reprice — was done in weeks while keeping a day job. A pivot is an MVP window too.)

The 30-day sprint, with this week's real numbers as calibration:

- **Week 1 — audit before you build.** If you have an existing flat product, run the Visualizee audit: pull 12 months of revenue, and if the line is flat, test whether the problem is demand (no one needs it) or friction (people need it but the interface and pricing make it hard). Interview 10 of your actual users or target buyers: what did they try before, what did they pay, what confused them? If you have no product yet, pick the outcome-first niche — one profession, one slow expensive step. Cost: $0.
- **Week 2 — replace interface with conversation.** Take the hardest configuration step in your product idea and delete it; make the AI ask the questions instead (Vizzy's pattern: proactively ask style, lighting, material — or their equivalent in your niche). Add a three-tier subscription: low ($15) for trying, mid ($35) for regulars, high ($80) for teams. Cost: under $100 in API spend and hosting.
- **Week 3 — ship the buy button and one owned channel.** Obidowski's ecosystem philosophy applies: put the buy button up before you feel ready (his community's advice: "ship the buy button first, decide later"). Publish one SEO page targeting the exact phrase your profession types — "floor plan to render," "sketch to photorealistic," vertical by trade. Free tier for the first render, paid from the second. Cost: $0.
- **Week 4 — first dollar, then read the curve.** Target one paying subscriber at any tier. Keep billing on Stripe or RevenueCat with a public verification key — all three of this week's cases got their coverage and their exit options because a third party can read their numbers. Then set a rule from the counter-cases: if the curve is flat after 90 days, audit friction before you spend on ads; if it only moves when ads are on (Private Venture), you are renting growth, so price accordingly and keep CAC below three months of subscription. Cost: $0.

**Your first step this week:** open your own product analytics (or pick one idea and do this on competitors) and find the configuration screen — the settings page, the form, the node editor — that sits between "user arrived" and "user got value." Write down every question it asks. That list is your chat assistant's script, and deleting that screen is the exact move that took one solo founder from $150 a month to $8,600.

## Micro-Opportunities

1. **Chat-first pivots for abandoned niche tools.** Target: the hundreds of small AI tools from 2023-2024 with node-heavy interfaces and flat revenue — their owners are tired, and the playbook is now proven ($150 → $8,600 in six months). Entry: acquire or rebuild one tool for your own trade, swap the interface for a guided chat, reprice to subscriptions. Starting cost: $0-200 to validate on a free tier.
2. **Photoreal visualization for one underserved trade.** Target: landscape designers, kitchen remodelers, fence and deck installers — trades where clients can't picture the result and studios charge hundreds per render. This is Visualizee's model, verticalized below its price floor. Entry: one image-model pipeline + trade-specific prompt presets, $15-35/mo. Starting cost: ~$50/month in API spend.
3. **MCP servers for paid developer products.** Target: indie developer-tool buyers who live in Claude and Cursor — Native Templates bundles MCP servers with $59-149 files and cites them as a sale-listing asset. Entry: wrap your existing product's catalog in an MCP endpoint; sell templates, starters or audits around it. Starting cost: a weekend.
4. **Localized wellness/self-tracking apps for non-English markets.** Target: Japanese, Korean and German self-improvement audiences — Private Venture's top market is Japan on fully localized SwiftUI, with organic channels still untouched. Entry: one on-device tracker + AI companion, weekly $4.99 pricing, ASO-first. Starting cost: ~$25/year in Apple developer fees; ads optional.
5. **Acquiring flat-curve micro-apps at 0.5x revenue.** Target: listings like Private Venture ($22,500, 0.5x) — apps with paying subscribers, working billing, and untouched organic channels the owner never tried. Entry: buy small with cash you can lose, apply the Week-2 chat pivot and Week-3 SEO playbook. Starting cost: the listing price; sub-$25K deals exist weekly on the verification boards.

## Signals Worth Knowing

**TrustMRR turned one year old, and the verification layer is now a marketplace.** The platform — built by Marc Lou in roughly six hours in Cursor after Pieter Levels complained about fake revenue screenshots — launched October 30, 2025 and has since collected $409,915 of its own all-time revenue (Stripe-verified, November 11), raised its ad slots from $299 to $1,499 a month with every slot selling out within three days ($20,378 in that first drop), and facilitated 216 website transactions through its listings (ReaderMX retrospective, November 2026). **What it means for you:** connecting a read-only payment key on day one is no longer bookkeeping hygiene — it is a distribution surface (your listing ranks and gets read) and an exit rail (this week's Native Templates and Private Venture listings both priced off verified numbers). The verification badge does trust-building your landing page copy cannot.

**Solo founders run wider channel stacks than teams — and mostly without ads.** The AI Receipts' channel study (November 25, 2026) found the 5 one-person businesses that fill in their marketing channels tick a median of 11 channels each, against 3 for the 20 larger or unstated-team listings; podcasts and referral programs appear in 3 of 5 solo listings but in none of the other 20, and only 2 of 5 solo founders pay for ads at all. **What it means for you:** the solo advantage is breadth of cheap channels, not depth of budget — cover SEO, one short-video surface, a podcast appearance and a referral loop before you ever open an ad account, because Private Venture's listing price is what a business looks like when ads are the only door.
